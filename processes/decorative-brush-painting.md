### Rosemaling (`lumenary:rosemaling`, a style that rests on this process)

_From: The valleys of Norway; when: Eighteenth and nineteenth century; works best on: Warm pictures with bright and dark contrast._

Rosemaling, rose painting, grew up in Norwegian valleys where country painters decorated chests, bowls and walls in imitation of the grander baroque and rococo styles of the towns. It is built from only a few brush strokes: the C-scroll, the S-scroll, the teardrop petal and the leaf. The painter loads the brush with two colours so every stroke has a light edge and a dark one.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Lay motifs on a staggered grid of spacing g.
2. Choose the motif from the picture’s brightness v: a flower where v is above 0.62, a pair of scrolls from 0.32 to 0.62, and a spray of three leaves below that.
3. Make the size follow brightness too: R = g (0.3 + 0.34 v).
4. Pick the nearest of six folk paints (red, ochre, green, blue, cream and brick) to the picture’s colour, or use the picture’s own colours.
5. Shade every stroke from a light edge to a dark edge, on a teal, black, green or cream ground.

**The maths:**

$$\text{kind}=\begin{cases}\text{flower}&v>0.62\\ \text{scroll}&0.32<v\le0.62\\ \text{leaves}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},\qquad R=g\,(0.3+0.34\,v)$$

_Motif choice and size (each size is also multiplied by a random 0.92 to 1.08). Places with v below 0.07 (1.4 − d), where d is the density slider, are left bare._ (primitives: tone-bands, tone-mark-size)

$$r(t)=R\,(1-0.78\,t),\qquad \varphi(t)=\varphi_0\pm 1.35\,(2\pi)\,t,\qquad w(t)=R\big(0.34\,(1-t)^{0.8}+0.05\big)$$

_A scroll: a spiral inward over 1.35 turns, drawn as a ribbon that tapers from thick to thin._ (primitives: parametric-curve, variable-width-stroke)

$$\varphi_i=\frac{2\pi i}{n}-\frac{\pi}{2},\qquad i=0,\dots,n-1$$

_The directions of the n petals of a flower, starting from the top._ (primitives: star-rosette-motif)

**Dials:** `size` (Motif size) = 70 [36 to 130]; `petals` (Petals) = 6 [4 to 9]; `density` (How much is painted) = 75 [20 to 100]; `colour` (Paint) = "picture" (picture, classic); `ground` (Ground) = "teal" (teal, black, green, cream); `seed` (New painting) = 3.

**History:** The painted style spread across valleys such as Telemark, Hallingdal and Gudbrandsdalen, each with its own manner, and flourished from around the middle of the eighteenth century. After a decline it was revived in Norway, and Norwegian immigrants carried it to the American Midwest, where Per Lysne and others kept it alive at places such as Vesterheim in Iowa.
