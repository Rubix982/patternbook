### Risograph (`lumenary:risograph`, models this process)

_From: Japanese stencil duplicators; when: 1980s machine, art-print revival since the 2000s; works best on: Illustrations, portraits and landscapes with clear tonal masses._

A risograph prints one colour per pass, so its pictures are built from two or three overlapping inks with a grainy fill and a hint of misregistration. This treatment makes two plates from the picture, one for shadows and one for almost everything, lays ink as random grain instead of dots, and multiplies the inks so overlaps mix like real transparent inks.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Make plate one from the darkness raised to the power 1.35, which carries the shadows.
2. Make plate two from the darkness raised to the power 0.75, which carries almost everything, and read it from a position a few pixels away.
3. Compare each plate with a threshold of 0.5 plus a mix of white and smooth noise to decide where to ink, and smooth the comparison slightly.
4. Multiply the two inks together, so overlaps become a deeper third colour.
5. Multiply in a faint paper fibre pattern.

**The maths:**

$$a_1=(1-L)^{1.35},\qquad a_2=0.92\,\big(1-L(\mathbf x+\boldsymbol\delta)\big)^{0.75}$$

_The two plates; δ is the misregistration offset, about 7 px across and 4.5 px up at full strength._ (primitives: misregistration, tone-curve)

$$\text{cov}_k=\operatorname{smoothstep}\big(\vartheta_k-0.09,\ \vartheta_k+0.09,\ a_k\big),\qquad \vartheta=\tfrac12+\big(0.55\,r+0.45\,n-\tfrac12\big)G$$

_Coverage by ink k; r is white noise, n smooth noise, G = 0.25 + 1.3 × the grain slider._ (primitives: tooth-threshold, smooth-noise, seeded-random)

$$C=C_{\text{paper}}\prod_{k=1}^{2}\Big(1-\text{cov}_k\,\delta_k\,\big(1-I_k/255\big)\Big)$$

_Multiply blend of the two inks I_k on the paper; δ_k is the ink density slider times a faint patchy unevenness._ (primitives: multiply-blend)

**Dials:** `pair` (Inks) = "blue-pink" (blue-pink, teal-orange, green-red, purple-yellow, black-red); `grain` (Grain) = 60 [0 to 100]; `miss` (Misregistration) = 40 [0 to 100]; `density` (Ink density) = 90 [40 to 130]; `paper` (Paper) = "cream" (cream, white, grey); `seed` (Reprint) = 1.

**History:** The Japanese company Riso Kagaku developed the Risograph as a fast, cheap duplicator for schools and offices, and it reached the market in the 1980s. Illustrators and small presses adopted it in the 2000s for its soft colour, grain and low cost, and riso zines and prints are now a recognisable look.

## patternbook's model: `riso`

_The theory of patternbook's own `riso` treatment (`treatments/treatments.js`), "Two-colour risograph", moved here from `treatments/theory/riso.md` on 2026-11-22 so it sits with the process it models._

**For:** drawings in a two-ink style.
**Comes from:** Lumenary's Risograph treatment by Mursal Furqan Kumbhar
(`vendor/lumenary/src/engine/styles/risograph.js`; his theory in `lumenary/theory/06-craft.md`).
Adapted with his permission. His model, constants and tone curves; our colour separation.

> **C-013:** against `press` on C-011's winning page, the judge rated both equal on "less
> AI-made" and "convincingly printed", and called `riso` the more filtered of the two.

### Why it looks this way

A risograph is a stencil duplicator: one ink per pass, so two drum passes for two colours. It
doesn't lay ink as dots. It forces ink through a stencil, so tone shows up as random grain,
inking is uneven over broad patches, and the second pass never lines up with the first.

### What we changed from Lumenary's version

His treatment is for photographs. It makes its two plates from **brightness** (plate one from
shadows, plate two from almost everything) and offers fixed ink pairs. Our drawings already use
exactly two inks, so the plates come from `press`'s **colour separation** instead (black coverage
k, spot coverage s), and the inks are dials. Everything after that is his:

    a₁ = k^1.35,   a₂ = 0.92 · s(x + δ)^0.75          (his plate curves; δ = misregistration)
    θ = ½ + (0.55 r + 0.45 n − ½) · G,  G = 0.25 + 1.3 · grain/100
    coverage = smoothstep(θ − 0.09, θ + 0.09, a) · density · pat
    pat = 0.9 + 0.1 · n_broad + 0.06 · (r' − ½)        (broad patches of thicker and thinner ink)
    δ = (7, −4.5) · (miss/100) · W/800 px
    out = paper · Π(1 − coverage · (1 − ink)) · (1 + 0.05 · (r'' − ½))   (paper fibre)

r is per-pixel hash noise and n smooth value noise at 0.7 / (W/800) per pixel.

### How it differs from `press`

- Tints are **random grain**, not a dot screen.
- **No edge wobble or ink spread.** Edges stay where they were; only the grain roughens them.
- Ink is never quite solid: at density 90, a black line prints at about 80–95% black.
- Because the grain threshold clusters around ½, light tints print sparser than their nominal
  coverage even with the tone curves.

### Dials

`grain` (60), `miss` (40), `density` (90), `ink`, `spot`, `paper`, `seed`.
