### Madhubani (`lumenary:madhubani`, a style that rests on this process)

_From: Mithila, in Bihar and Nepal; when: Ancient tradition, on paper since the 1960s; works best on: Pictures with clear shapes and bold colour._

Madhubani, or Mithila, painting does not like empty space. Every figure is outlined and then filled with a pattern of its own, lines, dots, scales or waves, so that the painting hums with texture. The colours come from natural pigments, and women painted them on the walls of houses, especially for weddings. This treatment does the same by colour: a region of red gets a lattice, a region of yellow gets dots.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Blur the picture and snap it to eight pigments: madder red, turmeric, leaf green, indigo, lamp black, paper, saffron and earth.
2. Clean regions with a majority filter and outline them in black wherever two colours meet.
3. Choose a fill pattern for each pigment: a diamond lattice on red, dots on yellow, waves on green, fish-scale arcs on blue, dots in paper white on black, concentric rings on saffron and stripes in paper white on earth.
4. Set the pattern size and strength with the sliders.
5. Add a double border with its own small motifs, like the decorated edge of a wall painting.

**The maths:**

$$\Big|\operatorname{frac}\!\big(\tfrac{x+y}{q}\big)-\tfrac12\Big|<0.07\ \ \text{or}\ \ \Big|\operatorname{frac}\!\big(\tfrac{x-y}{q}\big)-\tfrac12\Big|<0.07$$

_The lattice of diagonal lines in red regions. Lines of width 0.14 q in each of two directions, so about 1 − 0.86² ≈ 26% of the region is covered._ (primitives: lattice-repeat)

$$\Big\lVert\operatorname{frac}\!\big(\tfrac{\mathbf x}{q}\big)-\tfrac12\Big\rVert<0.17\quad(\phi=\pi\cdot0.17^2\approx0.09)$$

_The dot pattern: a dot in every square. Its covering fraction φ is about 9%._ (primitives: lattice-repeat)

$$\operatorname{frac}(5\rho)<0.32,\ \ \rho=\Big\lVert\operatorname{frac}\!\big(\tfrac{\mathbf x}{1.6\,q}\big)-\tfrac12\Big\rVert<\tfrac12$$

_Concentric rings in saffron regions: five rings per unit of radius, each about a third of the ring spacing._ (primitives: lattice-repeat)

$$C\leftarrow C+\tau\,\big(C_{\text{line}}-C\big),\qquad \tau=0.9\,\kappa,\quad C_{\text{line}}=\begin{cases}\text{paper}&\text{black, earth}\\ \text{ink}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$

_The fill line is blended with strength 0.9 κ, where κ is the pattern strength slider (0 to 1), so light lines are drawn on the dark pigments and dark lines on the others._ (primitives: mix-over)

**Dials:** `simplify` (Simplify) = 5 [1 to 10]; `size` (Pattern size) = 7 [3 to 14]; `pattern` (Pattern strength) = 80 [0 to 100]; `line` (Outline) = 60 [0 to 100]; `frame` (Border) = 60 [0 to 100].

**History:** Madhubani painting comes from the Mithila region of Bihar and the neighbouring parts of Nepal, painted with twigs and cotton-wrapped sticks and with pigments from turmeric, indigo, soot, flowers and clay. After the Bihar earthquake of 1934 the British officer William Archer photographed wall paintings in damaged houses. From the 1960s women artists such as Sita Devi, Ganga Devi and Mahasundari Devi brought the art onto paper, and it became known across India and abroad.

### Warli (`lumenary:warli`, a style that rests on this process)

_From: The Warli people of Maharashtra and Gujarat; when: Ancient tradition, shown on paper from the 1970s; works best on: Pictures with a dark subject on a light ground._

Warli painting is made with a vocabulary of three shapes: a circle, a triangle and a square, which are said to come from the sun and the moon, from mountains and trees, and from a field or a hut. A person is two triangles meeting at their tips with a circle for a head and stick limbs, and a crowd of them dances in a ring. The picture decides where the crowd gathers.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Make a wall texture from two noises, a broad one 90 px across and a fine one 5 px across.
2. Place candidate positions on a staggered grid with spacing 0.82 u (u is the figure size), each shifted by up to a quarter of the spacing.
3. At each position let the picture’s tone t set the chance that anything is drawn: p = min(1, 1.15 d (1 − t)^1.5), where d is the crowd slider. Dark places hold a thick crowd and light ones stay empty.
4. Draw the figures in white rice paste: dancers, animals, trees and huts, with slightly wobbly lines.
5. Add a border of small triangles and squares.

**The maths:**

$$p(t)=\min\!\big(1,\ 1.15\,d\,(1-t)^{1.5}\big)$$

_The chance that a grid position holds a drawing, from the tone t and crowd slider d._ (primitives: importance-sampling, tone-curve)

$$N\approx\sum_{\text{sites}}p(t)\ \approx\ \frac{A}{(0.82\,u)^2}\ \overline{p}$$

_The expected number of drawings in an area A._ (primitives: importance-sampling)

$$s=u\,\big(0.8+0.4\,(1-t)\big)$$

_The size of a figure, from the tone._ (primitives: tone-mark-size)

$$\text{kind}=\begin{cases}\text{person}&h<0.60\\ \text{tree}&h<0.76\\ \text{bird}&h<0.86\\ \text{hut}&h<0.96\\ \text{sun}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$

_The pick, with h a random number fixed by the position and the seed. Figures are drawn from the top of the wall down so lower ones overlap higher ones._ (primitives: seeded-random)

**Dials:** `size` (Figure size) = 26 [14 to 46]; `crowd` (Crowd) = 72 [20 to 100]; `wall` (Wall) = "terracotta" (terracotta, ochre, night); `border` (Border) = true; `seed` (New crowd) = 1.

**History:** Warli painting belongs to the Warli people, an Adivasi community of Maharashtra and Gujarat. It is painted on the red-brown mud walls of houses, in white rice paste, with a bamboo stick, and is often made for weddings, around a square drawn for the goddess Palaghata. In the 1970s the artist Jivya Soma Mashe brought Warli painting onto cloth and paper, and received a Padma Shri in 2011.
