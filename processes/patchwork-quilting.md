### Quilt Patchwork (`lumenary:quilt-patchwork`, models this process)

_From: Textile traditions worldwide; when: Centuries old; works best on: Bold pictures with simple colour regions._

A quilt makes a picture from scraps: cut cloth into squares or triangles, choose the colour that best matches each part of the picture, and sew them together. The quilting stitches then pull each patch into a gentle puff, light on top and shadowed at the seams. Printed fabrics add pattern to some patches for extra life.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Divide the picture into squares, or split each square into two triangles along a randomly chosen diagonal.
2. Choose k fabric colours by k-means and give each patch the nearest to the picture’s colour at its centre.
3. Give a chosen fraction of patches a pattern in a lighter or darker shade of their own colour: dots, stripes, a check, florets or diagonal lines.
4. Puff each patch with a radial gradient, white at 20 percent near the middle and black at 30 percent at the rim.
5. Draw a thin dark seam and a dashed running stitch on a copy of the patch outline shrunk to 82 percent.

**The maths:**

$$s=\frac{W}{n},\qquad \text{patch colour}=\arg\min_{f\in F}\big\lVert \bar P(\text{centre})-f\big\rVert$$

_Patch size for n patches across, and its fabric._ (primitives: coarse-grid, palette-snap)

$$\text{pattern}\iff h<\pi,\qquad \text{kind}=\Big\lfloor \frac{5\,h}{\pi}\Big\rfloor$$

_A patch is printed when its random number h is below the printed-fabrics slider π (0 to 1); the kind of print is chosen from five._ (primitives: seeded-random)

**Dials:** `across` (Patches across) = 13 [6 to 30]; `shape` (Patches) = "triangles" (squares, triangles); `fabrics` (Fabrics) = 8 [4 to 12]; `prints` (Printed fabrics) = 60 [0 to 100]; `stitch` (Running stitches) = true; `seed` (Re-cut) = 1.

**History:** Patchwork and quilting appear in many cultures, from the kantha of Bengal and the ralli of Sindh to the quilts of Britain and North America, where they used up worn clothing as well as celebrated skill. The Quilts of Gee’s Bend, made by a community of women in Alabama, were shown in major museums in the 2000s and brought wide attention to the craft as art.

### Ralli Patchwork (`lumenary:ralli-patchwork`, models this process)

_From: Sindh and its neighbouring deserts; when: Traditional, made by women over generations; works best on: Bright pictures with clear blocks of colour._

A ralli is a quilt made from scraps. In Sindh it is a women’s art: cloth from old clothes is cut into squares and triangles, laid out in bold patterns and stitched down in rows of running thread, often in several layers. Nothing is wasted, and the result is warm, bright and personal. Your picture is cut into patches, and each takes a colour from a bright palette.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Make a square grid of side s. When the diamond set is chosen, turn each point by 45° to (u, v) = ((x + y), (y − x))/√2 before applying the grid, so squares stand on their points.
2. Keep each square whole, cut it into two triangles by a diagonal, or into four by both.
3. Give each piece the colour of the picture at its own centre, with saturation raised by 1.5, snapped to the nearest of nine cloth colours (or an earthy set).
4. Print some pieces as cotton with dots, stripes, checks or diamonds.
5. Stitch dashes 4.6 px inside every seam, to hold the layers together.

**The maths:**

$$u=\frac{x+y}{\sqrt2},\qquad v=\frac{y-x}{\sqrt2},\qquad (i,j)=\big(\lfloor u/s\rfloor,\ \lfloor v/s\rfloor\big)$$

_Cell index on the diagonal grid. With “square on” the rotation is skipped._ (primitives: lattice-repeat)

$$d_{\text{diag}}=\frac{|a+b|}{\sqrt2}\ \ (\text{halves}),\qquad d_{\text{diag}}=\frac{\big||a|-|b|\big|}{\sqrt2}\ \ (\text{quarters}),\qquad a=f_u-\tfrac12,\ b=f_v-\tfrac12$$

_The distance, in cell units, from the cut line. (f_u, f_v) is the position inside the cell. |a + b|/√2 is the distance from a point to the line a + b = 0._ (primitives: sdf-shape)

$$d_{\text{seam}}=s\,\min\!\big(d_{\text{edge}},\,d_{\text{diag}}\big),\qquad f=0.82+0.2\,\operatorname{smoothstep}\big(0,\,10\,\mathrm{sc},\,d_{\text{seam}}\big)$$

_Distance to the nearest seam and the puffing of each patch: 18% darker at the seam._ (primitives: sdf-shape, soft-threshold)

$$\big|d_{\text{seam}}-4.6\,\mathrm{sc}\big|<0.55\,\mathrm{sc}\ \ \wedge\ \ \operatorname{frac}\!\Big(\frac{0.8x+1.3y}{6.5\,\mathrm{sc}}\Big)<0.55$$

_The stitch: a band 1.1 px wide, broken into dashes (55% on)._ (primitives: running-stitch)

**Dials:** `size` (Patch size) = 62 [22 to 110]; `layout` (Set) = "diamond" (diamond, square); `split` (Cut) = "halves" (whole, halves, quarters); `cloth` (Cloth) = "bright" (bright, earth); `print` (Printed cotton) = 60 [0 to 100]; `stitch` (Stitching) = 75 [0 to 100].

**History:** The word is said to come from a verb meaning to mix, which suits a cloth made of many colours. Rallis are made in Sindh and in the neighbouring regions of Thar, Cholistan and Kutch, and are often made as gifts and for a daughter’s wedding. Each family has its own colours, and the ralli remembers the clothes it was made from.
