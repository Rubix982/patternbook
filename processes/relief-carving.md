### Marble Museum (`lumenary:marble-museum`, models this process)

_From: Stone relief sculpture; when: Antiquity onward; works best on: Portraits and figures lit from one side._

A relief is sculpture that stays attached to its wall. The carver has only a few centimetres of depth, so form is suggested by slopes that catch the light. Marble adds its own character: it lets light sink in a little, which softens every shadow, and it is crossed by veins that ignore the carving completely. One spotlight in a dark room does the rest.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Use the picture’s brightness, mostly blurred, as the height of the stone.
2. Find the slope at each pixel and light it from the spotlight’s direction.
3. Darken the hollows and add a polished highlight.
4. Colour the stone with soft clouding, and draw veins where a warped noise crosses its middle.
5. Hang the slab on a dark wall with a shadow, lit edges and a brass plate.

**The maths:**

$$h=(1-\tfrac12 q)\,B_{7}^{2}L+\tfrac12 q\,B_{1.5}L$$

_Height: broad forms plus a share q of fine detail (the chisel slider, 0 to 1)._ (primitives: tone-to-height, blur)

$$I=\operatorname{clamp}\big(0.36+0.84\,(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf l),\ 0.2,\ 1.3\big)\cdot\Big(1-\operatorname{clamp}\big(3.2\,(\bar h-h),\,0,\,0.5\big)\Big)$$

_Shading: soft diffuse light times the darkening of hollows; h̄ is the local average height._ (primitives: relief-shading)

$$\text{vein}=\operatorname{clamp}\Big(1.1\,\big(1-\lvert 2N_1-1\rvert\big)^{9}+0.5\,\big(1-\lvert 2N_2-1\rvert\big)^{12}\Big)$$

_N₁ and N₂ are warped fractal noises at two scales; the fold makes a ridge where each crosses one half._ (primitives: smooth-noise)

**Dials:** `depth` (Depth of carving) = 55 [10 to 100]; `detail` (Fine chisel work) = 50 [0 to 100]; `veins` (Veins) = 50 [0 to 100]; `polish` (Polish) = 50 [0 to 100]; `stone` (Stone) = "carrara" (carrara, nero, rosso, verde); `light` (Spotlight from) = 235 [180 to 360]; `title` (Brass plate) = "Untitled, marble"; `wall` (Gallery wall) = true; `seed` (Another block) = 1.

**History:** Relief is among the oldest forms of sculpture, from Egyptian tomb walls to the marble frieze of the Parthenon, carved around 440 BC. In fifteenth-century Florence Donatello developed rilievo schiacciato, “flattened relief”, in which a scene is modelled in a depth of millimetres. White marble from Carrara in Tuscany has been quarried since Roman times and was the stone of Michelangelo.

### Aztec Glyphs (`lumenary:aztec-glyphs`, a style that rests on this process)

_From: The Mexica of central Mexico; when: Fourteenth to sixteenth century; works best on: Pictures with strong light and dark, which raise and lower the relief._

The Mexica carved in stone, and the best known piece is the Sun Stone, a disc whose rings hold the twenty signs for the days of the ritual count. Twenty signs run against thirteen numbers to give the 260 days of the sacred year. Brighter places in your picture stand higher out of the stone here, and a raking light gives each glyph a shadow.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Make a wall of square tablets, each with a raised plate, a raised border and one of eleven glyphs: the spiral of wind, the five-dot movement sign, the stepped fret, the sun, the serpent, the flower, water, the flint, the house, the face and an eight-pointed star.
2. Set the height of the plate to 0.18 + 0.4 t where t is the picture’s brightness, with the border adding 0.22 and the glyph 0.42.
3. Paint the heights on a hidden grey canvas, blur it by about a pixel and light it as a relief, from the slope of the surface.
4. Choose the light direction in degrees, and the stone: basalt, sandstone or limestone.
5. Add traces of pigment and weathering that gather in the hollows.

**The maths:**

$$H=\rho\,h_{\text{canvas}}+0.25\,\bar L,\qquad h_{\text{plate}}=0.18+0.4\,t$$

_The height map: ρ is the depth slider, h_canvas the drawn heights and L̄ a broad blur of the picture’s brightness, which gives the whole wall a gentle swell._ (primitives: tone-to-height)

$$f=\operatorname{clamp}\big(0.74+0.7\,(\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf l),\ 0.3,\ 1.45\big)\,(1-a),\qquad \mathbf s=-k\,\nabla H$$

_Relief lighting: l = (cos α, sin α) is the direction to the light and k = 22 + 70 ρ scales the slope._ (primitives: relief-shading)

$$a=\operatorname{clamp}\big(2.4\,(\bar H-H),\ 0,\ 0.55\big)$$

_Occlusion: where the height H is below its own blur H̄ (a groove) the surface is darkened by up to 55%._ (primitives: relief-shading)

**Dials:** `size` (Tablet size) = 72 [40 to 130]; `relief` (Depth) = 65 [20 to 100]; `light` (Light direction) = 225 [0 to 360]; `paint` (Pigment) = 60 [0 to 100]; `wear` (Weathering) = 45 [0 to 100]; `stone` (Stone) = "basalt" (basalt, sandstone, limestone); `seed` (New glyphs) = 4.

**History:** The Sun Stone, often called the Aztec calendar, was found in Mexico City in 1790. The 260-day count, the tonalpohualli, combined twenty day signs with thirteen numbers. Mexica carvers decorated temples, altars and sculpture, and the glyphs are still studied by archaeologists and honoured by Nahua communities today.

### Hieroglyphs (`lumenary:hieroglyphs`, a style that rests on this process)

_From: Ancient Egypt; when: About 3200 BCE to the 4th century CE; works best on: Pictures with warm and cool contrast._

The Egyptians cut their writing in two ways. In raised relief the background is cut away and the signs stand out. In sunk relief the signs are cut into the surface, which lets them be read in the strong light of an outside wall, where a hollow throws a clear shadow. Both were painted, in lapis blue, red ochre, malachite green, yellow ochre and black. The signs here are chosen for how they look and do not spell a message.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Write in columns divided by double rules, with a horizontal band every six lines, and face signs alternately in neighbouring columns, since hieroglyphs face the start of the line.
2. Draw twelve signs: the ankh, the eye, water, the feather, the sun disc, the djed pillar, the was sceptre, a snake, a duck, an owl, a lotus and a basket.
3. Cut each in with a dark stroke on a middle-grey plate, so the lighting finds a hollow.
4. Make the signs fuller and the pigment stronger where the picture is bright.
5. Light the relief from a chosen direction and add weathering to limestone, sandstone or granite.

**The maths:**

$$H=\rho\,\big(0.59-0.49\,\chi\big)+0.18\,\bar L,\qquad \chi\in\{0,1\}\ \text{inside a sign}$$

_Sunk relief: the plate is at 150/255, a sign is cut down to 25/255 (a depth of 0.49 ρ) and L̄ is a broad blur of brightness._ (primitives: tone-to-height)

$$u=\tfrac{s}{2}\,\big(0.5+0.5\,t\big)$$

_The half-size of a sign in a cell of height s, from the picture’s brightness t._ (primitives: tone-dot-lattice)

$$f=\operatorname{clamp}\big(0.74+0.7\,(\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf l),\ 0.3,\ 1.45\big)\,(1-a),\qquad \mathbf s=-k\,\nabla H$$

_Lit as in the Aztec relief: with the light from the chosen direction and an occlusion term a that darkens the grooves._ (primitives: relief-shading)

**Dials:** `size` (Sign size) = 48 [24 to 90]; `relief` (Depth) = 65 [20 to 100]; `paint` (Paint) = 70 [0 to 100]; `light` (Light direction) = 225 [0 to 360]; `wear` (Weathering) = 40 [0 to 100]; `stone` (Stone) = "limestone" (limestone, sandstone, granite); `seed` (New text) = 6.

**History:** The Rosetta Stone, found in 1799, carried the same decree in hieroglyphs, Demotic and Greek. Jean-François Champollion announced the decipherment in 1822. Egyptian writing was used for more than three thousand years, and the last known inscription, at Philae, dates from 394 CE.

### Makli Carving (`lumenary:makli-carving`, models this process)

_From: The Makli hills near Thatta, Sindh; when: Fourteenth to eighteenth century; works best on: Pictures with bright and dark regions._

The Makli necropolis is one of the largest burial grounds in the world, and its tombs are carved in sandstone and limestone with rosettes, lozenges, arcades and flowing foliage. The soft stone has been worn by the sun, by rain and by the salt in the air. This treatment fills the page with a repeating cell of relief and lights it like a wall, with the picture setting how deep the carving is.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Build the relief as a height map. In the eight-petal rosette the flower’s outer edge is at radius p(θ) = 0.8 (0.52 + 0.48 |cos 4θ|), the petals rise to 0.62 with a thin groove along the middle, a boss rises to 0.95 at the centre, a ring surrounds the flower, and corner ornaments rise to 0.55.
2. Use a diamond plate, a groove, a centre boss and corners for the lozenge pattern.
3. Multiply the height by 0.12 + 1.15 T², where T is the blurred brightness, so bright regions are deeply cut and dark regions are shallow.
4. Light the height map from the chosen direction and shade by its slope.
5. Add weathering, which wears the carving and stains the stone, and tint the stone from the picture.

**The maths:**

$$p(\theta)=0.8\,\big(0.52+0.48\,|\cos4\theta|\big),\qquad h_{\text{petal}}=0.62\,\operatorname{smoothstep}\big(-w,\,w,\,p(\theta)-\rho\big)$$

_The rosette’s outline and petal height. w = 0.05 is the softness of the edge, and ρ the distance from the cell’s centre (cell coordinates from −1 to 1)._ (primitives: star-rosette-motif, sdf-shape)

$$H=P\,\big(0.12+1.15\,T^2\big)+0.9\,T_s+\eta$$

_The final height map: P is the carved pattern, T the picture’s blurred brightness, T_s a much broader blur (which gives a gentle swell) and η a fine noise._ (primitives: tone-to-height)

$$f=\operatorname{clamp}\!\big(0.74+0.7\,(\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf l),\ 0.3,\ 1.45\big)\,(1-a),\qquad a=\operatorname{clamp}\big(2.4\,(\bar H-H),\,0,\,0.55\big)$$

_Lighting by the slope s = −k∇H (k = 70 × depth) against the light direction l, and occlusion a where the height is below its own blur H̄._ (primitives: relief-shading)

**Dials:** `pattern` (Carving) = "rosette" (rosette, lozenge); `size` (Cell size) = 64 [40 to 150]; `relief` (Depth) = 60 [20 to 100]; `light` (Light direction) = 225 [0 to 360]; `tint` (Picture in the stone) = 45 [0 to 100]; `weather` (Weathering) = 50 [0 to 100]; `stone` (Stone) = "sandstone" (sandstone, limestone, red).

**History:** Makli, near Thatta, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1981. Its tombs and mausoleums were built from the fourteenth to the eighteenth centuries under the Samma, Arghun, Tarkhan and Mughal rulers. Visitors are struck by the stone carving and by the glazed tile, and conservation work continues against the weather, salt and neglect.
