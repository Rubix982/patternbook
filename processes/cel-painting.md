### Anime Cel (`lumenary:anime-cel`, a style that rests on this process)

_From: Hand-painted cel animation; when: 1914 patent, television anime from 1963; works best on: Portraits and characters with clear shapes._

An animator has to paint the same character thousands of times, so the picture is simplified until that is possible. Every area gets one flat colour and at most one shadow tone with a hard edge, outlines are inked in a thin even line, and a white shape marks whatever shines. Those economies became a style, and the style outlived the paint.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Smooth the picture with a filter that flattens areas and leaves outlines where they are.
2. Find a small palette of paints and give every pixel the nearest one.
3. Add one shadow tone, as a hard step where the picture is darker than its surroundings.
4. Ink lines on the dark side of edges, between unlike paints, and on small dark features such as eyes.
5. Paint white shine on spots that are very bright and brighter than what is round them.
6. Screen a blurred copy over the frame for a soft glow.

**The maths:**

$$p'_i=\frac{\sum_j w_{ij}\,p_j}{\sum_j w_{ij}},\qquad w_{ij}=\exp\!\Big(-\frac{\lVert p_i-p_j\rVert_1^{2}}{2\,\sigma^{2}}\Big),\quad\sigma=76.5$$

_Edge-keeping smoothing over the pixels j within the reach of pixel i, along one row or column at a time._ (primitives: bilateral-smooth)

$$\mathbf f=\big(Y,\ 1.7\,(B-Y),\ 1.7\,(R-Y)\big),\qquad Y=0.299R+0.587G+0.114B$$

_The space in which paints are found and matched._ (primitives: palette-snap, kmeans-palette)

$$s=S_{\tau,\,\tau+0.014}\big(L_{16}-L_{2}\big),\qquad \tau=\operatorname{lerp}(0.12,\ 0.02,\ \text{shadow})$$

_The cel shadow: L with a wide blur minus L with a narrow one, cut to a hard step._ (primitives: dog-lines, soft-threshold)

**Dials:** `colours` (Paint colours) = 13 [6 to 20]; `smooth` (Shape smoothing) = 45 [0 to 100]; `shade` (Cel shadow) = 55 [0 to 100]; `lines` (Ink lines) = 60 [0 to 100]; `vivid` (Colour) = 45 [0 to 100]; `shine` (Shine) = 30 [0 to 100]; `glow` (Soft glow) = 30 [0 to 100]; `ink` (Line colour) = "tinted" (tinted, dark, brown, blue).

**History:** Earl Hurd patented the use of clear celluloid sheets for animation in 1914: characters were inked on the front, painted on the back and photographed over a background. Japanese television animation began in earnest with Astro Boy in 1963, whose tight budget made flat colour and limited movement a virtue. Studios moved to digital ink and paint around 2000. In 2006 Winnemöller, Olsen and Gooch showed how to get the look from video in real time, with the same steps used here.

### Retro Anime (`lumenary:retro-anime`, a style that rests on this process)

_From: Japanese animation shot on film; when: 1980s to mid 1990s; works best on: Night scenes and anything with lights or reflections._

Animation of the 1980s was painted on cels and photographed onto film, then seen on a tube television from a tape. Each step left something: paint that never reached true black, outlines photocopied a little too heavy, a halo round every light, stars on every sparkle, grain, and colour smeared sideways by the video signal. Those marks are the nostalgia.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Flatten the picture into cel paint with heavy outlines.
2. Lift the darkest tones toward navy and warm the lightest toward cream.
3. Break the outlines up a little, as a photocopy does.
4. Blur the highlights widely and add them back as a warm halo.
5. Shift red one way and blue the other, and soften each row.
6. Add grain, darken the corners and draw star glints on the brightest spots.

**The maths:**

$$C\leftarrow C+(C_{\text{navy}}-C)\cdot0.5f\,\big(1-S_{0,\,0.5}(l)\big),\qquad C\leftarrow C+(C_{\text{cream}}-C)\cdot0.4f\,S_{0.6,\,1}(l)$$

_Ageing of a paint of lightness l; f is the fade slider._ (primitives: soft-threshold)

$$R'(x)=R(x-b),\qquad G'(x)=G(x),\qquad B'(x)=B(x+b),\qquad b=\operatorname{round}(3\cdot\text{bleed})$$

_Colour bleed along each row._ (primitives: misregistration)

$$C\leftarrow C+(C_{\text{halo}}-C)\cdot0.75\,k\cdot B_{9}^{2}\,S_{0.72,\,0.95}(Y)$$

_The halo: bright parts, blurred twice with a box of radius 9, blended toward a warm white; k is the halo slider._ (primitives: glow-bloom, soft-threshold)

**Dials:** `colours` (Paint colours) = 11 [6 to 16]; `fade` (Pastel fade) = 55 [0 to 100]; `lines` (Outlines) = 65 [0 to 100]; `halo` (Halo on lights) = 55 [0 to 100]; `bleed` (Colour bleed) = 40 [0 to 100]; `grain` (Film grain) = 45 [0 to 100]; `glints` (Star glints) = 5 [0 to 12]; `seed` (New print) = 1.

**History:** The 1980s were a boom for Japanese animation. Home video created a market for direct-to-tape releases from 1983, and feature films such as Akira (1988) reached audiences abroad. Cels were shot one frame at a time on a rostrum camera, and effects were made in camera: lights were cut as holes in black card and lit from behind, which gave their glow. By the late 1990s computers had replaced cel, camera and film.
