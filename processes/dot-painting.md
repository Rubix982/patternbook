### Pointillism (`lumenary:pointillism`, a style that rests on this process)

_From: Paris, Neo-Impressionism; when: 1880s and 1890s; works best on: Sunlit landscapes and scenes with soft colour._

Seurat’s idea was to stop mixing paint on a palette and put pure dots of colour side by side, leaving your eye to mix them. Seen from a distance, blue and yellow dots give a vibrating green that is brighter than mixed paint. This treatment throws thousands of dots of the picture’s own colours onto a plain ground and lets the mixing happen in the viewer.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Blur the picture to about the scale of one dot.
2. Throw dots at random positions and sizes, enough that a pixel lies under two to six of them on average.
3. Give each dot the colour of the blurred picture under its centre, lifted in saturation.
4. Wander each dot a little in hue, saturation and lightness, with up to 7 percent taking a complementary hue for the flecks that make shadows vibrate.
5. Let some of the plain ground show through where dots happen not to overlap.

**The maths:**

$$n=\frac{(2+4c)\,W\,H}{\pi\,r^{2}}$$

_Number of dots for coverage slider c (0 to 1) and nominal radius r._ (written for this model: specific)

$$P(\text{bare})\approx e^{-\lambda},\qquad \lambda\approx 0.96\,(2+4c)$$

_Chance that a pixel is under no dot at all (the 0.96 allows for the spread of dot sizes)._ (written for this model: specific)

$$h'=h+0.09\,v\,(u_1-\tfrac12),\quad s'=s\,(1.08+0.3\,v)+0.04,\quad l'=l+0.2\,v\,(u_2-\tfrac12)$$

_The jitter in hue, saturation and lightness; v is the colour jitter slider and u₁, u₂ are random numbers in [0, 1]._ (primitives: saturation-adjust, seeded-random, hue-shift)

**Dials:** `dot` (Dot size) = 38 [0 to 100]; `cover` (Coverage) = 65 [0 to 100]; `vivid` (Colour jitter) = 55 [0 to 100]; `ground` (Canvas) = "cream" (white, cream, dark); `seed` (Re-dot) = 1.

**History:** Georges Seurat painted A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte in 1884 to 1886, and Paul Signac helped develop the method, called divisionism or pointillism. They drew on nineteenth century colour theory, including Michel Eugène Chevreul’s work on simultaneous contrast. The principle returns every time an image is made of dots, from halftone printing to a screen’s pixels.

### Aboriginal Dot Painting (`lumenary:aboriginal-dot-painting`, a style that rests on this process)

_From: The Western Desert, Australia; when: Acrylic painting from 1971; works best on: Landscapes, which fit the contour rings._

This treatment borrows only a visual technique, rows of dots in earth colours on dark ground. A real work carries what a picture cannot: the stories and law of a Country, which belong to the people and families who hold them. Dots were used in part to cover the meaning of a design from people who were not meant to read it. Please read this as a tribute and not as a copy, and look for the real paintings.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Blur the picture’s brightness heavily to a smooth field F.
2. Find the contour lines where F crosses each of n evenly spaced levels, using marching squares, and chain the pieces into curves.
3. Place dots of radius R along each curve every 2.3 R of arc length, with a little jitter.
4. Colour each ring from the ochre palette by level, in bands, or from the picture.
5. Fill the ground between rings with scattered small dots, on black, red or night ground.

**The maths:**

$$\{\mathbf x:\ F(\mathbf x)=\ell_i\},\qquad \ell_i=0.07+0.86\,\frac{i+\tfrac12}{n}$$

_The ring i is the contour of the blurred brightness F at level ℓᵢ._ (primitives: iso-contours)

$$\Delta=2.3\,R,\qquad N\approx\frac{\Lambda}{\Delta}$$

_Dots sit Δ apart along a contour of length Λ, so a longer ring simply holds more of them._ (written for this model: layout)

$$\delta x\approx\frac{0.86}{n\,\lVert\nabla F\rVert}$$

_The spacing between neighbouring rings: it shrinks where the gradient of F is large, which is why dots crowd at edges._ (primitives: iso-contours)

$$j=\min\big(5,\ \lfloor 6\,\ell\rfloor\big)$$

_Dot colour by light level: ℓ picks one of six ochres from deep red up to cream._ (primitives: tone-bands)

**Dials:** `dot` (Dot size) = 5 [3 to 10]; `rings` (Rings) = 11 [5 to 18]; `fill` (Ground dots) = 55 [0 to 100]; `colour` (Colours) = "level" (level, bands, picture); `ground` (Ground) = "black" (black, red, night).

**History:** In 1971 men of several language groups at Papunya, in the Northern Territory, began to paint on boards and canvas, with the encouragement of a schoolteacher, Geoffrey Bardon, the designs that belong to their Country and its stories. The Papunya Tula Artists company followed in 1972. Today Aboriginal artists across Australia, working through their own art centres, are among the best known painters in the world.
