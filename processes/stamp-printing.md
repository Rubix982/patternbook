### Rubber Stamp (`lumenary:rubber-stamp`, models this process)

_From: Offices and mail rooms; when: Late 19th century onward; works best on: High contrast pictures and bold silhouettes._

A rubber stamp prints one flat colour or nothing, so the picture is cut to black and white and the interest comes from imperfection: uneven pressure, worn patches, pinholes and a slightly crooked angle. Those flaws are what make a stamp look pressed by a person.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Cut the picture to black and white at the chosen threshold, after a small random wobble so the edge is ragged.
2. Add a double border and a banner with the word cut out of it on the same plate.
3. Remove ink with a slow noise for uneven pressure, a mid-scale noise for worn patches, and scattered single-pixel pinholes.
4. Rotate the inked layer by the tilt angle.
5. Combine it with the paper by multiplication, as ink soaking into fibres darkens what is beneath.

**The maths:**

$$\text{inked}\iff L_b+0.12\,(n-\tfrac12)+0.05\,(r-\tfrac12)<\tau,\qquad \tau=\tfrac{\text{cut}}{100}$$

_Where the stamp carries ink; n is smooth noise and r a random number._ (primitives: tooth-threshold, smooth-noise, seeded-random)

$$\alpha=0.96\ \kappa\ \big(0.62+0.38\,n_1+0.14\,(n_2-\tfrac12)\big)\ \chi_{\text{wear}}\ \chi_{\text{pin}}$$

_Ink opacity: κ is 1 on the plate; χ is 0.1 on worn patches and 0 on pinholes._ (primitives: smooth-noise)

$$C_{\text{out}}=C_{\text{paper}}\cdot\big(1-\alpha\,(1-C_{\text{ink}}/255)\big)$$

_Multiply blend of ink and paper._ (primitives: multiply-blend)

**Dials:** `word` (Stamped word) = "APPROVED"; `ink` (Ink) = "red" (red, blue, black, green); `cut` (Where the ink goes) = 52 [25 to 75]; `wear` (Worn rubber) = 45 [0 to 100]; `tilt` (Tilt) = -4 [-15 to 15]; `seed` (Press again) = 1.

**History:** Seals pressed into clay or wax are thousands of years old. The rubber stamp became practical after Charles Goodyear’s vulcanisation of rubber in 1839 and was in common office use by the late nineteenth century, to date, approve and file. Today stamps are a staple of crafts, scrapbooking and printmaking.

### Adinkra (`lumenary:adinkra`, a style that rests on this process)

_From: The Akan peoples of Ghana and Côte d’Ivoire; when: Nineteenth century and earlier; works best on: High contrast pictures._

Adinkra symbols are visual sayings. Each one stands for a proverb or a value: the heart for patience and tolerance, the fern for endurance, the ram’s horns for strength and humility. They are stamped in dark ink in squares on cloth, so a cloth is a page of sayings, and here the symbols grow larger and heavier where your picture is dark.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Carry ten symbols drawn with strokes and fills: rings, heart, sankofa, horns, star, fern, twist, comb, handcuffs and tree.
2. Divide the cloth into squares with three-line rules made with a comb, as on the real cloth.
3. In each square read the shade v of the picture, its darkness on light cloth or its brightness on dark cloth, and stamp a symbol sized by v.
4. Add the ink’s slight uneven pressure and let the picture tint the cloth.
5. Choose ink from bark brown, picture colour or gold, on cream, indigo, red or ochre cloth.

**The maths:**

$$u=0.45\,s\,\big(1-f+f\,(0.3+0.7\,v)\big),\qquad \text{stamp only if } v\ge 0.12$$

_Half the stamp size: s is the square size and f the size-follows-shade slider._ (primitives: tone-dot-lattice)

$$\rho_{\text{ink}}\propto\frac{u^2}{s^2}\propto\big(0.3+0.7\,v\big)^2$$

_The share of a square covered by ink, for filled symbols._ (primitives: tone-dot-lattice)

$$a=\operatorname{clamp}\big(0.55+0.9\,n(\mathbf x),\,0,\,1\big)$$

_Uneven inking: the alpha of the stamped layer is scaled by a noise n of wavelength about 5 px._ (primitives: smooth-noise)

$$C_{\text{cloth}}\leftarrow C_{\text{cloth}}\,\big((1-\beta)+\beta\,C_{\text{picture}}\big)$$

_A multiply blend with strength β = 0.7 × the tint slider, which dyes light cloth with the picture. Indigo and red cloth use a screen blend at 0.35 instead._ (primitives: multiply-blend)

**Dials:** `size` (Stamp size) = 56 [30 to 100]; `follow` (Size follows shade) = 80 [0 to 100]; `tint` (Picture in the cloth) = 45 [0 to 100]; `rules` (Printed rules) = 60 [0 to 100]; `cloth` (Cloth) = "cream" (cream, indigo, red, ochre); `ink` (Ink) = "bark" (bark, picture, gold); `seed` (New order) = 3.

**History:** Adinkra cloth was first worn at funerals, and the word is often given as meaning goodbye. Symbols are carved into pieces of calabash and stamped on cotton with a dark dye boiled from tree bark. Tradition links the name to Nana Kwadwo Agyemang Adinkra, a king of Gyaman. Symbols such as Sankofa, which says to go back and fetch what you forgot, are now used on buildings, jewellery and logos.
