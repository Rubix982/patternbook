# Primitives

The operations that recur across processes, each written once with its maths (`README.md`; `docs/architecture.md`
section 7b). A treatment or generator is written by composing them, and a process that needs one that doesn't exist
yet names the gap: the last section lists the gaps the subcontinent's crafts show.

**How this was built (C-018, 2026-11-22).** The vocabulary was seeded from Lumenary's six foundation pages and the
shared helper functions in his engine (`engine/`), extended from a sample of 43 of the 214 models
across every class, and frozen at 53 primitives before any equation was tagged (the frozen copy is
`runs/c018/vocab-v1-frozen.md`). All 610 equations of the models were then tagged
(`models/equation-tags.tsv`). 18 primitives were added after the freeze because three or more models turned
out to apply them; each says why. Lumenary's maths is Mursal Furqan Kumbhar's (credited in every model file); the
names, grouping and tags here are patternbook's.

**How to read an entry.** *Maths* gives the operation's canonical form and where it was first seen; *Helpers* names
the shared functions in Lumenary's code that implement it, if any; *Applied by* (generated by
`tools/process_models.mjs`, between markers) lists the models whose equations apply it, and the processes they belong
to. A primitive is **shared** when three or more models apply it.

**What was measured.** 489 of the 610 equations (80 %) apply a shared primitive; 31 shared primitives are enough to
reach 70 % of all equations. The share is 66 % once foundation-level operations with no craft content (random
numbers, smoothstep, tone curves, blur, simple blends) are set aside, and highest in the making classes (printing,
drawing, photographic, stitch, carving, glaze: 87-94 %) and lowest in geometric constructions (59 %). The equations
undercount the sharing in the code: blur, for one, is imported by 89 treatments and written in 17 models' equations.
The full account is `runs/c018/primitives-summary.md`; the card is C-018 in `CARD.md`.

## Vocabulary v1 (frozen before tagging)

### luminance
**Name:** brightness from RGB (Rec. 709 weights)
**Plain words:** turns a colour into one number for how bright it is, weighting green most and blue least.
**Maths:** $L=\dfrac{0.2126R+0.7152G+0.0722B}{255}$ — foundation *Pixels, brightness and resampling*.
**Helpers:** `lumOf`, `lumArray`, `pixels`.
**Entered:** v1 (seed, foundation).

### coarse-grid
**Name:** coarse-grid sampling (pixelate: each cell is the average of its patch)
**Plain words:** shrinks the picture to a small grid by repeated halving, so each cell, dot, letter or stitch carries the average colour of the patch it stands for.
**Maths:** $p'_{x,y}=\tfrac14\left(p_{2x,2y}+p_{2x+1,2y}+p_{2x,2y+1}+p_{2x+1,2y+1}\right)$, repeated; a cell's colour $\bar P_{ij}$ is the mean over the cell — foundation *Pixels, brightness and resampling*.
**Helpers:** `scaleCanvas`, `sampleImage`, `fitSize`; `sampler` (smoothed colour looked up at a cell centre).
**Entered:** v1 (seed, foundation).

## What the subcontinent's crafts need that the primitives don't yet cover

_Since C-018: warp and weft from a draft is covered by `weave-draft` (C-019); supplementary weft, both kinds, by
`supplementary-weft` (C-020); darning on the cloth's own threads by `counted-darning` (C-021), except a bagh's full
coverage (T-022); chain stitch, on straight and curved paths and in concentric fill, by `chain-stitch` (C-022); running stitch in rows
following a contour, and the ripple of quilting, by `contour-rows` and `quilt-ripple` (C-023); mordant and resist
dyeing in an ordered sequence of baths by `dye-baths` (C-024), except indigo's depth under madder (T-023), the width of
a dabu's veins (T-024) and wicking measured along the threads (T-025); the mirror inset, its ring of shisha stitch,
its grid and its glint by `mirror-inset` (C-025), except likeness to real openings and glints (T-026, T-027); a glaze
with thickness, cobalt seen and spread through it, and sgraffito cut to the clay by `glaze-layer` (C-026), except slip
trailed as a raised line (T-028 for likeness); inlay cut from chosen stone and fitted to the motif by `inlay-cut`
(C-027), except the stone's polish as a specular response, and whether cutters really turn the bands (T-029); a
pierced screen's thickness, for the sun's light and the eye, by `thick-screen` (C-028), except undercut relief at
several depths (T-030 for proportions). Block printing's slipped and unevenly pressed impressions are in `dye-baths`
(C-024)._

Each verdict says how far the shared primitives (or the unshared ones, where named) cover the operation. It is based on the equations and, where an equation note was not enough, on the treatment's code.

| operation | verdict | what covers it | what is missing |
|---|---|---|---|

No primitive takes a weave draft (which warp is up at each pick: plain, twill, satin) and works out where warp or weft shows. Tapestry's visible warp is a bespoke gap term. Celtic-knot's over/under parity is one model. |
| **Weaving structures:** supplementary weft (jamdani, brocade) | not covered | kente's float blocks are the nearest: `seeded-random` decides whether a block is a float | An extra weft that floats over the ground only inside a motif and is tied down at intervals: discontinuous, motif by motif, in jamdani; selvedge to selvedge, often in zari, in brocade. |
| **Stitch paths:** running | covered | `running-stitch` (felt-story, ralli-patchwork, sashiko) | Only along straight lines, seams and edge insets. Rows that follow a curved motif, as in kantha, would need `streamline-trace` and `running-stitch` together, and no treatment combines them. |
