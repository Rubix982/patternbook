### Paper Cut-out (`lumenary:paper-cut-out`, models this process)

_From: Folk paper cutting and modern collage; when: Centuries old, cut-out painting in the 20th century; works best on: Landscapes and portraits with soft tonal masses._

A paper cut-out landscape is a stack of layers, each a sheet cut with scissors, each throwing a soft shadow on the one below. If you cut the picture’s brightness into bands like a contour map and stack coloured sheets, the depth is created by shadow alone. Swapping the colours changes the whole mood without changing the shapes.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Blur the picture’s brightness to round off detail.
2. Make layer i exist wherever brightness is above the threshold i divided by n, with a very short smoothstep at the edge for a sharp cut.
3. Colour layers from dark at the bottom to light at the top, from a palette or from the picture’s own colours.
4. For each layer, shift its mask a few pixels away from the light, blur it twice, and darken the sheets beneath wherever that shadow falls outside the layer.
5. Composite from the bottom up, add a hairline of light at the cut edges and multiply in paper fibre.

**The maths:**

$$m_i=\operatorname{smoothstep}\big(\tfrac in-0.012,\ \tfrac in+0.012,\ L_b\big),\qquad i=1,\dots,n-1$$

_The mask of layer i, with n layers in all._ (primitives: tone-bands, soft-threshold)

$$s_i=\operatorname{blur}^{2}\big(m_i(\mathbf x-\mathbf d)\big),\qquad \mathbf d=5\,\sigma\,(-\cos\alpha,\ \sin\alpha)$$

_Shadow of layer i: the mask displaced away from the light (angle α) and blurred twice._ (primitives: blur)

$$C\leftarrow\big(C\,(1-0.55\,\delta\,s_i\,(1-m_i))\big)(1-m_i)+c_i\,m_i$$

_Compositing one layer; δ is the shadow slider and c_i the colour of paper i._ (primitives: mix-over, multiply-blend)

**Dials:** `palette` (Papers) = "sunset" (sunset, ocean, forest, mono, picture); `layers` (Layers) = 5 [3 to 7]; `smooth` (Smoothness of cuts) = 50 [0 to 100]; `shadow` (Shadow) = 65 [0 to 100]; `angle` (Light from) = 135 [0 to 360].

**History:** Paper cutting is a folk art in China, Poland, Mexico and many other places. In the 1940s and 1950s Henri Matisse, limited by illness, cut and arranged painted paper in large works such as The Snail, and Lotte Reiniger made silhouette animated films from cut paper in the 1920s. Layered paper shadow boxes are now a popular craft.

### Papel Picado (`lumenary:papel-picado`, models this process)

_From: Mexico; when: Centuries old, with tissue paper from the 19th century; works best on: Bright, simple pictures with warm colour._

Papel picado means punched paper. Banners of tissue are strung across streets and altars for fiestas and for the Day of the Dead, and they flutter in the wind, so the light through the holes keeps moving. Holes are the whole drawing here: the more light a place in your picture has, the wider the cut, so the picture shows through the openings.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Draw each banner as a rectangle of width w and height 1.18 w, with a zigzag lower edge of seven teeth and a fold at the top where the string runs.
2. Hang rows from straight lines, shift alternate rows by half a banner and tilt each by a few hundredths of a radian.
3. Cut by painting in destination-out mode, so cuts remove paper and show what is behind.
4. Add a border of small holes and a 7 by 7 lattice in the middle, where each hole is sized by the blurred brightness of the picture at that cell.
5. Colour the tissue from a fiesta, pastel or marigold palette, or from the picture, with a see-through setting.

**The maths:**

$$r=\frac{c}{2}\,\big(0.1+0.95\,t^{0.85}\big)\,\big(0.5+h\big),\qquad r\le 0.62\,c$$

_Hole radius in a lattice cell of side c: t is the picture’s brightness there and h the hole size slider (0.3 to 1). Holes under 0.9 px are skipped._ (primitives: tone-dot-lattice, tone-curve)

$$\phi\approx\frac{\pi r^2}{c^2}=\frac{\pi}{4}\,\big(0.1+0.95\,t^{0.85}\big)^2\,(0.5+h)^2$$

_The fraction of each cell that is open, for round holes. It is how much of the picture shows through._ (primitives: tone-dot-lattice)

$$C=(1-\alpha)\,C_{\text{paper}}+\alpha\,C_{\text{picture}},\qquad \alpha=0.55\,\sigma$$

_The tissue is a little see-through: σ is the see-through slider._ (primitives: mix-over)

**Dials:** `size` (Banner width) = 84 [44 to 150]; `holes` (Hole size) = 72 [30 to 100]; `shape` (Holes) = "mixed" (mixed, circle, diamond, flower); `colour` (Tissue) = "fiesta" (fiesta, pastel, marigold, picture); `tissue` (See-through) = 25 [0 to 100]; `backdrop` (Behind) = "bright" (bright, dusk, mono).

**History:** Paper cutting in Mexico has roots in the bark paper (amate) of the Aztecs and in tissue that crossed the Pacific on the Manila galleons from the sixteenth century. The banners are made today by stacking fifty or so sheets and tapping steel chisels through them with a mallet. Papel picado is a symbol of Día de Muertos, on the first and second of November, when it decorates altars for the dead.

### Chinese Paper Cut (`lumenary:chinese-paper-cut`, models this process)

_From: China; when: More than a thousand years old; works best on: Bold silhouettes and faces._

A paper-cut is made from one sheet, so every part has to stay joined to the rest. Fine detail therefore comes from slits, crescents and teeth rather than loose islands. Dark areas stay solid, midtones turn to lace and bright areas open out completely, which gives a graded picture from nothing but cuts. Red is the colour of luck.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Compute darkness v = 1 − L from the blurred brightness L.
2. Cut away the paper where v is below a lower threshold T₀.
3. Above it, let a cut amount k rise smoothly as the picture gets lighter, deciding how wide each slit or how big each crescent is.
4. Write each pattern (slits, crescents, teeth or dots) as a simple condition on the pixel position with a pattern size q.
5. Add a border of cuts and a shadow so the paper looks lifted from the background.

**The maths:**

$$k=\operatorname{smoothstep}\big(T_1+0.3,\ T_0,\ v\big),\qquad T_1=\tfrac{p}{100},\ \ T_0=T_1-\tfrac{f}{100}$$

_The cut amount: p is the “paper left” slider and f the fine cutting range. Below T₀ everything is cut._ (primitives: soft-threshold)

$$\Big|\operatorname{frac}\!\Big(\tfrac{x+y+\frac{q}{2}\sin\frac{x}{1.8q}}{q}\Big)-\tfrac12\Big|<\tfrac{k}{2}$$

_A slit: diagonal stripes with a slight wobble. A stripe of width k leaves exactly a fraction 1 − k of the paper._ (primitives: tone-line-screen)

$$\operatorname{frac}\!\big(\tfrac{y}{q}\big)<0.92\,k\,(1-\tau_w),\qquad \tau_w=2\Big|\operatorname{frac}\!\big(\tfrac{x}{q}+\tfrac{\lfloor y/q\rfloor}{2}\big)-\tfrac12\Big|$$

_Teeth: rows of triangles whose height grows with k._ (primitives: lattice-repeat)

**Dials:** `cut` (Paper left) = 52 [15 to 85]; `fine` (Fine cutting range) = 30 [5 to 60]; `pattern` (Cuts) = "slits" (slits, crescent, teeth, dots); `size` (Cut size) = 8 [4 to 16]; `frame` (Border) = 70 [0 to 100]; `paper` (Paper) = "red" (red, black, gold).

**History:** Paper was invented in China, and the oldest surviving paper-cuts, found in tombs at Turpan, date from about the sixth century. Windows are decorated with paper-cuts at the Spring Festival, and the character for double happiness is cut for weddings. Chinese paper-cut was added to UNESCO’s list of intangible cultural heritage in 2009.
