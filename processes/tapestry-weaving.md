### Tapestry Weave (`lumenary:tapestry-weave`, models this process)

_From: Weaving workshops of Europe, the Middle East and the Americas; when: Many centuries; works best on: Landscapes and simple scenes with clear colour regions._

In tapestry the coloured weft is passed back and forth over a hidden warp and packed so tightly that the warp disappears and the picture is built of horizontal rows. A pass of wool is round, so it is bright in its middle and dark where it dips, and hand beaten rows never run quite straight. These details turn a neat mosaic into something woven.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Divide the picture into rows (passes of weft) and into cells about as wide as a row is tall, and take one colour per cell.
2. Limit the colours to k wool colours by k-means.
3. Shift odd rows by half a cell, as in real weaving, and wave the row boundaries a little with the wobble slider.
4. Light each pass as a round strand, with a sine raised to the power 0.55, and alternate cells slightly in brightness to show the over and under.
5. Show the dark warp where each row dips, and draw loose warp ends as a fringe at the bottom.

**The maths:**

$$k=\big(0.34+0.8\,b\big)\,o\,\Big(0.9+0.1\sin\pi f_x\Big),\qquad b=\big(\sin\pi f_y\big)^{0.55}$$

_Brightness of the wool: f_y and f_x are the positions across the row and across the cell (0 to 1), o is 1 or 0.9 in a checkerboard._ (primitives: cylinder-shading)

$$k\leftarrow k\,\Big(1-\omega\,g\,\big(0.35+0.35\,w\big)\Big),\ \ w=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\sin\tfrac{2\pi x}{c},\qquad g=1-\operatorname{smoothstep}\big(0,\ 0.14,\ \min(f_y,1-f_y)\big)$$

_The warp showing through at the edges of a row; ω is the visible-warp slider, c the cell width and the sine-based factor the pattern of the hidden warp threads._ (primitives: soft-threshold)

**Dials:** `rows` (Passes of weft) = 84 [40 to 160]; `yarns` (Colours of wool) = 7 [3 to 12]; `warp` (Visible warp) = 55 [0 to 100]; `wobble` (Hand-woven wobble) = 45 [0 to 100]; `fringe` (Fringe) = true; `seed` (Re-weave) = 1.

**History:** Woven tapestries hung in medieval castles and cathedrals. The Gobelins manufactory in Paris was established in the seventeenth century, and the Renaissance painter Raphael designed cartoons in 1515 and 1516 for tapestries for the Sistine Chapel. In the twentieth century the Bauhaus weaving workshop, with artists such as Anni Albers, made tapestry a modern art.

### Navajo Weave (`lumenary:navajo-weave`, models this process)

_From: The Diné (Navajo) of the American Southwest; when: Centuries old, with rug trade from the 19th century; works best on: Bold pictures with strong shapes._

A Diné rug is woven on an upright loom, and every block of colour is a short run of weft pulled tight. An edge that is not square therefore has to step, so a diagonal becomes a staircase and a curve becomes a serrated line. The program does the same with a grid of yarn blocks laid over your picture, and its serrated edges are part of the look.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Lay a grid of cells (the yarn blocks) over the picture and give each the nearest colour of the chosen wool, or, for the natural greys, a grey chosen by brightness.
2. Create symmetry by folding the picture: for left-to-right mirroring the sample x becomes min(x, W − x), and for four-way symmetry y is folded as well.
3. Serrate edges by shifting the sample point a quarter of a cell one way on even rows and the other way on odd ones.
4. Frame the rug with a plain or stepped zigzag border.
5. Add fringe to the ends and subtle wool texture.

**The maths:**

$$x'=\min(x,\ W-x),\qquad y'=\min(y,\ H-y)$$

_Mirroring in the sample point: the right-hand side of the rug is a reflection of the left, and with four-way symmetry the bottom of the top._ (primitives: symmetry-fold)

$$z(a)=\min\big(a\bmod 8,\ 8-(a\bmod 8)\big),\qquad \text{red if } d-2\in\{z(a),\ 4-z(a)\}$$

_The stepped zigzag border. d is the distance in cells from the rug’s edge and a the cell’s position along it._ (primitives: lattice-repeat)

$$f=\Big(0.74+0.26\sin\big(\pi\,\operatorname{frac}(2\,y_c)\big)\Big)\,\big(0.93+0.14\,\xi\big),\qquad y_c=\tfrac{y-y_0}{c}$$

_Yarn: two ridges of weft per cell (y_c is the position across the cell) with a fuzz noise ξ._ (primitives: cylinder-shading, seeded-random)

**Dials:** `size` (Yarn block) = 9 [5 to 20]; `sym` (Symmetry) = "lr" (none, lr, quad); `palette` (Wool) = "ganado" (ganado, twogrey, sunset); `border` (Border) = "zig" (none, simple, zig); `serrate` (Serrated edges) = true; `fringe` (Fringe) = true.

**History:** Diné tradition says that Spider Woman taught the people to weave. Regional styles are known by their wool and colour: the red grounds of Ganado, whose style was encouraged by trader John Lorenzo Hubbell, and the natural greys and browns of Two Grey Hills. Weavers still work today, and the best way to honour the tradition is to buy from Diné weavers directly.
