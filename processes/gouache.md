### Gouache (`lumenary:gouache`, models this process)

_From: Illuminated manuscripts, miniatures and illustration studios; when: Medieval onward, mid 20th century illustration; works best on: Posters, landscapes and portraits with clear colour shapes._

Gouache is opaque and matte: the colour comes from the paint itself, not from the paper behind it, so a painter works from a few mixed pots and fills flat shapes. This treatment chooses a small palette from the picture, makes the colours a little chalky, and lets the shapes wobble where a hand and a wide brush would.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Find a palette by k-means clustering of the picture’s colours, then make each pot chalkier.
2. Blur the picture, label each pixel with its nearest palette colour, and tidy the regions with two passes of a majority filter.
3. Read each pixel’s label from a place displaced by smooth noise, so boundaries wobble the same way on both sides.
4. Show bare paper where a different label lies a few pixels away, the thin gap between flat areas.
5. Add long streaks of noise for brush drag and a few dry-bristle flecks.

**The maths:**

$$\lambda(\mathbf x)=\arg\min_{k}\ \lVert \mathbf c(\mathbf x)-\mathbf p_k\rVert^{2},\qquad s'=0.95\,s+0.02,\quad l'=\operatorname{clip}(0.86\,l+0.08,\,0.1,\,0.9)$$

_Each pixel takes the label of the nearest palette colour p_k; the palette itself is softened in saturation and lightness._ (primitives: palette-snap, saturation-adjust)

$$\mathbf x'=\mathbf x+2\,w\,\big(\boldsymbol\nu(\mathbf x)-\tfrac12\big)$$

_The wobble: ν is smooth 2D noise, w the hand-wobble slider (up to 5 px)._ (primitives: coordinate-warp, smooth-noise)

$$\text{bare}=0.9\,(0.6+0.4\,n)\quad\text{if}\ \ \lambda(\mathbf x'+g\,\mathbf e)\ne\lambda(\mathbf x')\ \text{for some}\ \mathbf e\in\{\pm\hat x,\pm\hat y\}$$

_The gap of paper between two flat areas; g is the gaps slider in pixels and n fine noise._ (primitives: label-boundary)

**Dials:** `colours` (Colours of paint) = 6 [3 to 10]; `smooth` (Smoothness of shapes) = 55 [0 to 100]; `wobble` (Hand-wobbled edges) = 45 [0 to 100]; `gaps` (Gaps of bare paper) = 35 [0 to 100]; `streaks` (Brush drag) = 45 [0 to 100]; `seed` (New palette) = 1.

**History:** The word comes from the Italian guazzo. Opaque water-based paint was used in medieval manuscripts and in Indian and Persian miniature painting, and gouache became the favourite of poster artists and illustrators in the twentieth century. Henri Matisse painted sheets of paper with gouache to make his cut-outs, and mid-century concept artists such as Mary Blair used it for bold colour.

### Persian Miniature (`lumenary:persian-miniature`, a style that rests on this process)

_From: Persia and the Timurid, Safavid and Mughal courts; when: Thirteenth to seventeenth century and later; works best on: Portraits, gardens and scenes with clear shapes._

A Persian miniature is a small painting made for a book. The painter uses flat areas of mineral colour, draws fine ink outlines, and does not cast shadows. Space is made by stacking things up the page, and meadows are pricked with tufts of grass and small flowers. Its jewel-like colour and its calm, flat world are the opposite of a photograph, and the recipe is easy to follow.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Blur the picture (simplify × 1.1 px), make it 25 percent more saturated, and snap each pixel to one of nine pigments by the redmean distance.
2. Remove specks with a majority filter run twice over a 5 by 5 window.
3. Draw outlines in dark brown ink where the colour index changes, with a strength of 0.8 × the ink slider.
4. Use fine parallel hatching for depth where the picture is dark, in place of shadows.
5. Add gold leaf, tufts of grass and a frame of gold rules and a blue band with gold teardrops on ivory paper.

**The maths:**

$$f=0.93+0.14\,t-\eta\,0.34\,\operatorname{smoothstep}(0.42,\,0.15,\,t)\,\big[\operatorname{frac}\!\big(\tfrac{0.7x+y}{3.1\,\mathrm{sc}}\big)<0.26\big]$$

_Brightness of the flat colour. The second term is the hatching: η is the hatching slider, and it only applies where the tone t is below 0.42._ (primitives: tone-line-screen)

$$C\leftarrow(1-\lambda)\,C+\lambda\,C_{\text{ink}},\qquad \lambda=0.8\,\iota\ \ \text{on an edge}$$

_Ink outline with strength λ, where ι is the ink slider (0 to 1) and C_ink is the dark brown (42, 26, 16)._ (primitives: label-boundary, mix-over)

$$r_{\text{gold}}=(1+1.6\,\xi)\,\mathrm{sc}\,(0.6+0.6\,\gamma),\qquad \alpha=0.5+0.5\,\gamma$$

_Size and opacity of a fleck of gold: ξ is a random number and γ the gold slider._ (primitives: seeded-random)

$$m=\operatorname{round}\big(0.1\,\min(W,H)\,\varphi\big),\qquad \text{lapis band at inset }0.64\,m,\ \text{width }0.5\,m$$

_The frame. It is dropped when m is under 8 px._ (written for this model: layout)

**Dials:** `simplify` (Simplify) = 4 [1 to 9]; `ink` (Ink outline) = 60 [0 to 100]; `hatch` (Hatching) = 45 [0 to 100]; `gold` (Gold leaf) = 55 [0 to 100]; `frame` (Frame) = 70 [0 to 100].

**History:** The pigments included lapis lazuli from the mines of Badakhshan, vermilion, malachite green, turmeric yellow and burnished gold leaf. Painting for books flourished at the courts of Tabriz, Herat and Isfahan, with masters such as Kamal ud-Din Behzad in the fifteenth century, and illustrated epics such as the Shahnameh. The Mughal court developed its own school, blending Persian and Indian traditions.
