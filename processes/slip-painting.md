### Greek Vase (`lumenary:greek-vase`, models this process)

_From: Ancient Greece: Corinth and Athens; when: Black-figure from about 700 BC, red-figure from about 530 BC; works best on: Pictures with strong shapes and clear dark or light figures._

Greek vase painters had no colours to mix, only one black glaze and the red of the clay. So every figure had to be a silhouette, and detail had to be scratched or drawn as line. That limit made the style: bold shapes, the profile view, and a border of repeating pattern to hold the picture in. A photograph pressed through it loses its shading and keeps its outline, which is what a silhouette is for.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Lift the local contrast, then cut the picture into two tones at a level set by how much is to be painted.
2. In black-figure the dark shapes are glaze on bare clay; in red-figure the light shapes are the clay and the ground is glazed.
3. Scratch fine lines through the glaze to the clay for black-figure, or draw them in glaze on the clay for red-figure.
4. Add red and white over the glaze where the picture is warm or cool, as painters did for cloth and skin.
5. Draw a meander border above and below, shade the pot darker towards its sides, and age it with stains and cracks.

**The maths:**

$$T=\ell+0.85\,(\ell-\bar\ell),\qquad G=\operatorname{smoothstep}(t+0.035,\ t-0.035,\ T)$$

_Black-figure: the glaze mask G is where the boosted brightness T falls below the threshold t._ (primitives: unsharp-mask, soft-threshold)

$$\text{shade}=1-0.62\,c\,|2u-1|^{2.3}$$

_The curve of the pot: u is the position across the picture (0 to 1) and c the curve slider._ (primitives: cylinder-shading)

$$\text{crack}=1-\operatorname{smoothstep}\big(0.5\sigma,\ 1.5\sigma,\ 0.9\,(d_2-d_1)\big)$$

_A net of cracks from a Voronoi pattern: d_1 and d_2 are the distances to the nearest and second nearest points, and sigma is the picture scale._ (primitives: crackle-network)

**Dials:** `mode` (Technique) = "black" (black, red); `figures` (How much is painted) = 50 [0 to 100]; `incise` (Scratched detail) = 60 [0 to 100]; `clay` (Clay) = "attic" (attic, corinth, pale); `accent` (Added red and white) = true; `border` (Meander borders) = true; `curve` (Curve of the pot) = 45 [0 to 100]; `age` (Age and wear) = 40 [0 to 100]; `seed` (Another pot) = 1.

**History:** Black-figure pottery began in Corinth about 700 BC and was perfected in Athens, where painters drew figures in a slip that turned black in a three-stage firing and scratched details through to the clay. Around 530 BC the Andokides Painter and his circle reversed it: red-figure left the figures the colour of the clay and painted the ground, so that details could be drawn with a brush. The meander, or Greek key, is a running hook pattern and among the oldest decorations of the Greek world.
