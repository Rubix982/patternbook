### Shadow Puppet (`lumenary:shadow-puppet`, models this process)

_From: Java and Bali, China, India, Turkey; when: More than a thousand years; works best on: A dark figure in profile against a light background._

A shadow puppet is not looked at, it is looked through. The audience sits on the far side of a lit cloth and sees only what the figure blocks, so the maker draws with holes: rows of small punched openings let the lamp through and pick out a jewel, a fold or an eye. Held close to the cloth the shadow is sharp; drawn back, it softens and grows.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Make everything darker than a threshold into one solid figure.
2. Lay a slanted lattice of points over the figure.
3. Punch a round hole wherever the figure is lighter than its surroundings, and in bands that follow its forms.
4. Cut slits along its strongest inner edges.
5. Blur the figure by its distance from the cloth.
6. Light a woven cloth from behind with one lamp, let a little colour through the hide, and add the rods.

**The maths:**

$$\theta=L_{(\lceil q\,N\rceil)},\qquad m=1-S_{\theta-0.015,\ \theta+0.015}(L_w)$$

_The figure: θ is the brightness at rank qN among all pixels, L_w the blurred brightness._ (primitives: tone-bands, soft-threshold)

$$\text{hole at }(g_x,g_y)\iff L_f-L_m>0\ \ \text{or}\ \ \big\lvert\operatorname{frac}(k\,L_m)-\tfrac12\big\rvert<0.15$$

_Punch rule at a lattice point: L_f is a fine blur, L_m a wide one, k between 5 and 11 bands._ (primitives: lattice-repeat, dog-lines, iso-contours)

$$t=S_{0,1}\!\Big(1-\frac{\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf x_{\text{lamp}}\rvert}{R}\Big),\qquad C=\operatorname{ramp}_{\text{edge}\to\text{warm}\to\text{hot}}(t)$$

_The lit cloth; R is 62% of the frame’s diagonal._ (primitives: colour-ramp, vignette)

**Dials:** `cut` (How much is figure) = 50 [15 to 85]; `holes` (Pierced pattern) = 60 [0 to 100]; `soft` (Distance from the cloth) = 30 [0 to 100]; `tint` (Painted hide) = 25 [0 to 100]; `cloth` (Light) = "lamp" (lamp, moon, rose); `rods` (Rods) = true; `seed` (Light the lamp again) = 1.

**History:** Shadow theatre is old across Asia. In Java and Bali, wayang kulit figures are cut from buffalo hide, pierced with fine chisels and painted, and a single puppeteer, the dalang, performs epics through the night by the light of an oil lamp; UNESCO recognised it in 2003. Chinese shadow puppetry flourished in the Song dynasty and uses translucent coloured hide. India has several leather puppet traditions, and Turkey has Karagöz.

### Mughal Jali (`lumenary:mughal-jali`, models this process)

_From: Mughal India and Pakistan; when: Sixteenth to eighteenth century; works best on: Landscapes and scenes with distance._

Jali means a net or a lattice. The Mughals carved them from single slabs of marble and red sandstone to screen windows, tombs and balconies. They let in air and a broken light and hide the people behind them. Standing in front of one, you see the world through a pattern, and the sun throws that pattern in shadow across the floor. Here the stone is a slab with openings, your picture is the scene behind, and the sun throws the lattice across it.

**How it is made, as the model does it:**

1. Describe each opening with a margin function in pixels that is positive inside the hole and negative in the stone.
2. For the eight-point star, take the star’s polar radius (the same star polygon formula as the Ajrak page) minus the distance from the cell’s centre, combined with a diamond at the corners.
3. For the six-point star, split the plane into hexagons: turn the point (x, y) into hexagonal coordinates, round to the nearest hexagon and draw the star about it. The lotus has its own petal outline.
4. Shade the stone with a bevel whose depth follows the thickness slider, and cast the shadow of the lattice across the scene in the chosen sun direction.
5. Let the scene glow where the light falls through the holes.

**The maths:**

$$q=\frac{\frac{\sqrt3}{3}x-\frac{y}{3}}{\sigma},\qquad r=\frac{\frac23\,y}{\sigma},\qquad (q,\,-q-r,\,r)\ \to\ \text{nearest integer triple summing to }0$$

_Hexagonal (cube) coordinates for the six-point star lattice, σ = 0.62 s. After rounding each part, the one with the largest rounding error is reset so the three still add up to zero._ (primitives: lattice-repeat)

$$\mathrm{margin}=\max\big(m_1,\,m_2\big)\,\frac{s}{2},\qquad m_1=r_\star(\theta)-\rho,\quad m_2=0.34\,h-\big[(1-|u|)+(1-|v|)\big]$$

_The eight-point opening in a cell with coordinates u, v from −1 to 1. r⋆ is the star radius with R₁ = 0.92 h and R₂ = 0.5 h, and h = 0.45 + 0.55 × the opening slider. Union of two shapes is the maximum of their margins._ (primitives: sdf-shape, star-rosette-motif)

$$M=\operatorname{smoothstep}\big(-0.9,\,0.9,\,\mathrm{margin}\big),\qquad \text{lit}=M_b\big(\mathbf x+\mathbf l\,d\big),\quad d=26\,\mathrm{sc}\,\tau$$

_The mask, and the sun lookup: τ is the thickness slider, l the unit vector of the sun’s direction and M_b the blurred mask._ (primitives: sdf-shape, blur)

$$f_{\text{view}}=(0.38+0.9\,g)\,(0.42+0.7\,\text{lit})$$

_How bright the scene is behind an opening: g is the light slider._ (written for this model: specific)

**Dials:** `pattern` (Carving) = "star8" (star8, star6, lotus); `cells` (Cells across) = 7 [3 to 16]; `hole` (Opening) = 78 [30 to 100]; `depth` (Thickness of the stone) = 55 [0 to 100]; `light` (Sun direction) = 225 [0 to 360]; `glow` (Light on the view) = 65 [0 to 100]; `stone` (Stone) = "marble" (marble, sandstone, blush).

**History:** Jali screens are found in Shah Jahan’s marble screen round the cenotaphs at the Taj Mahal and in the windows of Salim Chishti’s tomb at Fatehpur Sikri, and the tradition continued in the Rajput palaces such as the Hawa Mahal in Jaipur, built in 1799. They served as ventilation, privacy and decoration at once, and they cooled interiors by softening the sun. Modern architects have returned to the idea for facades in hot climates.
