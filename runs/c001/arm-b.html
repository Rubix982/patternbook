CHAIN

Replace a bicycle chain with a quick link

1

EN

Warning!

A chain that is joined badly can snap under load and throw you off the bike.

Check the quick link after step 9 and again after your first short ride. If any link is stiff or bent, or the quick link is not fully locked, do not ride the bike. Take it to a bike shop.

EN

Caution!

Keep fingers away from the chain and chainring whenever the pedals turn.

A turning chainring pulls fingers in between chain and teeth. Turn the pedals only with your hand on the pedal or crank arm. Wear gloves: chain plates have sharp edges and the grease is hard to wash off.

EN

Note

Use a chain and quick link made for the number of cogs on your rear wheel.

Count the cogs on the rear cassette (for example 11). Many quick links can be used only once, so fit the new one that comes with the new chain. In step 5 the quick link counts as one link. If the old chain was the wrong length, or you are also changing the cassette, ask a bike shop to size the chain.
