Bike repair, at home

Replace your bike chain

Check the old chain, take it off, size the new one to your bike, thread it through the gears, and join it. Eight steps. The only move you can't undo is cutting the new chain to length, and step 4 covers it.

  • Time 30–45 min the first time
  • Difficulty moderate
  • Cost one chain, one quick link

    What you need

    Before you buy: two facts about your bike

    1. How many gears at the back?

    Count the cogs on the rear wheel. 8, 9, 10, 11 or 12 cogs means you need an 8-, 9-, 10-, 11- or 12-speed chain. Higher counts use narrower chains, so they don't mix.

    2. How is the chain joined?

    Most chains join with a quick link, which you open by hand or with pliers. Some use a joining pin pressed in with a chain tool. The new chain's box tells you which.

    1

    Check the old chain is worn

    A chain stretches as its pins wear. A worn chain then wears the cogs.

    1. With a chain checker: drop both ends into the chain. If the gauge sinks in fully at the marked limit, the chain is worn.
    2. With a ruler: rest it along the top run, put a pin on 0, and find the pin nearest 12 in.
    Pin at 12 in mark sits…What it means
    on the markChain is fine. Clean and lube it; no need to replace.

    Done when: you know the chain is past its limit, or you've decided it can stay.

    2

    Shift down and find the joint

    Slack chain is easier to open.

    1. Turn the pedals by hand and shift to the smallest cog at the back and the smallest ring at the front.
    2. Hang the bike on a stand, or flip it onto its seat and bars.
    3. Turn the pedals backwards slowly and look along the chain for one outer link that looks different: a different colour, an arrow, or small oval slots. That is the quick link.
    4. No quick link? The chain was joined with a pin. You'll push out any pin with the chain tool in the next step.

    Done when: the chain hangs loose and you have the joint (or a spot to break it) under your fingers.

    3

    Take the old chain off

    Before it comes off, take a phone photo of how it runs through the rear derailleur.

    1. Quick link: fit the pliers' tips into the rollers on either side of the quick link and squeeze. No pliers: grab the chain either side and push the two ends toward each other until it gives.
    2. No quick link: set any link in the chain tool, wind the handle until the pin is almost out, then flex the chain to free it. You're throwing this chain away, so any link will do.
    3. Pull the chain off the cogs and out of the rear derailleur. Keep it nearby: it is your guide for length in the next step.

    Done when: the old chain is in your hand and the derailleur hangs empty.

    4

    Find the right length

    New chains come long. You'll remove links so it fits your gears.

    1. Easiest: count links. Lay the old and new chains side by side on the floor and match them link for link, not by length (the old one has stretched). Mark the matching link on the new chain.
    2. If the old chain was the wrong length, or you changed gears: shift the front derailleur to the big ring, then wrap the new chain around the largest front ring and the largest rear cog, skipping the rear derailleur. Pull it snug where the ends meet, then add 1 in: one inner and one outer link.
    3. Remember that the quick link counts as an outer link. If you're using one, the chain must end in an inner link at both ends.
    4. Some single-front-ring bikes and full-suspension bikes need a different allowance. If the derailleur's instructions give a length method, use theirs.
    Stop and checkCount again before step 5. You can always take out one more link, but you cannot put a cut one back. If in doubt, leave it one inch long.

    Done when: one link on the new chain is marked (tape or a marker) as the place to cut, and both ends of the kept part will be inner links.

    5

    Cut the new chain

    The chain tool pushes a pin out of an outer link.

    1. Seat the pin you're removing in the tool's outer cradle (the one farther from the handle), with the chain's rollers sitting in the cradle teeth.
    2. Wind the handle until the driving pin touches the chain pin. Check it's centred, then keep winding until the pin comes fully out.
    3. Lift away the cut-off piece. Check that the chain you kept ends in an inner link at each end (for a quick link).

    Done when: the chain is at the length you marked and both ends are narrow inner links.

    6

    Thread the chain through the gears

    Work from the front of the bike to the back, on the drive side.

    1. If the chain has writing on the plates, or the box says it is directional, keep the writing facing out, away from the wheel.
    2. Feed one end through the front derailleur cage and lay it over the smallest front ring, letting it hang off the front.
    3. At the back, lay it over the smallest cog, then down through the rear derailleur: in front of the upper pulley, then round the back of the lower pulley, forming an S.
    4. Check the chain sits between both cage plates of the rear derailleur and isn't caught on the small metal tab between the pulleys. Compare against your photo from step 3.
    5. Bring both ends together on the lower run, below the chainstay.

    Done when: both ends meet on the lower run and the chain follows the S through the derailleur, with no twist.

    7

    Join the ends

    Lift the chain off the front ring onto the frame first, so you have slack to work with.

    1. Quick link: push one half through one chain end from the back, the other through the other end from the front. Hook each pin into the opposite plate's slot.
    2. Pull the chain apart either side of the link until the pins slide to the narrow end of the slots.
    3. Lift the chain back onto the small front ring. Put the quick link on the top run, hold the rear brake, and press down firmly on a pedal. You should hear or feel a click.
    4. Joining pin instead: push the special new pin in by hand, press it through with the chain tool until it stands out the same amount as its neighbours, then snap off the guide with pliers.
    Stop and checkNever rejoin a chain with the original pin you pushed out; it will not hold. Some quick links are single-use: if yours says so, use a new one each time the chain is opened.

    Done when: the joint flexes freely, the pins sit at the narrow ends of both slots, and the link looks like its neighbours from both sides.

    8

    Test before you ride

    Turn the pedals by hand, then ride slowly.

    1. Stiff links: pedal backwards slowly and watch the chain pass through the rear derailleur. A link that makes the lower pulley jump is stiff. Flex it side to side until it moves freely.
    2. Biggest gears: shift to the largest front ring and largest rear cog. The derailleur cage should still have some forward travel left; it must not be pulled straight.
    3. Smallest gears: shift to the smallest ring and smallest cog. The chain should not sag or touch itself at the lower pulley.
    4. All gears: run through every gear on the stand, then ride a short way and push hard on the pedals in a middle gear. No slipping, no jumping.
    5. Add a drop of lube to each roller, turn the pedals, and wipe off the excess from the outside.

    If it slips under hard pedalling with a new chain, the rear cogs are likely worn (see the 1% row in step 1).

    Done when: it shifts into every gear cleanly and holds when you stand on the pedals.
