About

Most of the job is waiting.

I grew up near the Hungarian plain and spent my twenties counting birds for a regional survey before I ever owned a long lens. That work taught me the thing I still care about most: knowing an animal's routine well enough that it forgets I'm there.

I work slowly, from hides and on foot, and I don't bait, call or use drones near nesting sites. If a picture would cost the animal its rest or its meal, I don't take it.

My images have run in natural history magazines and field guides, and I teach two small-group trips a year.

From the notebook

14 September 2026

Nine days for one heron

The bird fished the same channel every evening but always with its back to the light. On day nine the wind turned and so did it. The frame on this page is from the final twenty minutes.

2 June 2026

Why I stopped shooting at 1/4000

Freezing every feather made the pictures look like specimens. A little motion in the wingtips says more about the bird than perfect sharpness does.

20 February 2026

Cold batteries, warm hands

Notes on working at minus thirty: which kit failed, what I'd pack differently, and the small ritual of swapping batteries inside your jacket every forty minutes.
