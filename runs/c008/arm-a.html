What's going wrong?

Pick the closest match. Work through the steps in order and stop as soon as it's fixed. Most problems are solved by step 2 or 3.

    Restart or factory reset?

    They sound alike but do very different things. Always try a restart first.

    Safe · 3 minutes

    Restart (power cycle)

    1. Unplug the power cable from the back of the router.
    2. If you have a separate modem, unplug that too.
    3. Wait a full 30 seconds.
    4. Plug the modem in first and wait until its lights settle.
    5. Plug the router in. Wait about 2 minutes for the Internet light to turn solid green.

    Your network name, password and settings stay exactly as they were.

    Erases settings

    Factory reset

    1. Leave the router powered on.
    2. Find the small RESET hole on the back.
    3. Press and hold it with a paperclip for 10 seconds, until all lights flash amber.
    4. Release. The router restarts in about 3 minutes.
    5. Connect to the network printed on the sticker underneath and set it up again.
    This removes your custom Wi-Fi name and password, parental controls and port settings. Every device in the house will need to reconnect.

    Where you put the router matters

    Wi-Fi gets weaker through walls, floors, water and metal. A better spot often beats any setting.

    • Central in the home, about chest height, out in the open.
    • Antennas pointing straight up, or one up and one sideways for multi-floor homes.
    • Inside a cabinet, behind the TV, or on the floor.
    • Next to a fish tank, mirror, microwave or cordless phone base.
    • In a far corner or the basement, if most of the house is upstairs.

    Common questions

    Where do I find my Wi-Fi name and password?

    If you've never changed them, they're printed on the sticker on the underside of the router, labelled Network (SSID) and Wi-Fi key. If you have changed them and forgotten, sign in to the router settings at 192.168.1.1 from a connected device, or do a factory reset.

    What's the difference between the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz networks?

    2.4 GHz reaches further and goes through walls better, but is slower and more crowded. 5 GHz is faster at short range. By default the R4 combines both under one name and moves devices between them automatically.

    Is the problem my router or my internet provider?

    Look at the Internet light. If it's red or off while the Power light is green, the router isn't receiving a connection from outside, which usually means the provider or the modem. If the Internet light is green but devices still can't load pages, the problem is more likely on the Wi-Fi side.

    How do I update the router's software?

    Updates install automatically overnight between 2 and 4 a.m. To check now, open the router settings, go to System → Firmware and press Check for update. Don't unplug the router while an update is running (Power light pulsing white).

    Should I restart the router regularly?

    You don't need to. If you find you're restarting it more than once a week to fix slowdowns, that's worth reporting to support, since it can point to overheating or a failing power adapter.
