## Judgment calls

- Attestation rule for colonial sources that cover undivided Punjab or the Bombay Presidency: an item counts for Pakistan only when the source names it next to a place now in Pakistan. Items Baden-Powell names without such a place were left out, among them salu and kharwa (red cloths), nilah (indigo cloth), gazzi, chautahi outside the khes lists, and shatranji as a separate element. Items he places in what is now India were also left out: ghati and bulbul-chashm damask (Jalandhar, Hoshiarpur, Patiala, Ludhiana), Rampur chaddars (Ludhiana), dastar (Ludhiana), kamkhwab (Nabha, Amritsar). Gazzi and Shatranji therefore stay unconfirmed, though Baden-Powell describes both.
- A notified GI is counted as attestation. Notification is a government list of names under the GI Act, with a provincial department as registrant; it is not a registration. Only three textile or dress GIs are registered (Sindhi Ajrak, Sindhi Topi, Peshawari Chappal).
- The three shawl GIs (Islampur, Chilasi, Thari) are separate elements, mirroring the first pass, which lists Kullu, Kinnauri and Naga shawls as elements. The carpet GIs (Thari, Jirga, Warrior, Korga) are branches of the hand-knotted carpet, mirroring the first pass, which put "Pakistani qaleen" and "Kashmir rug" there as aliases. Folding the three shawls into one woollen-shawl element would reduce (a) by 2.
- The woven lungi cloths used as turbans and scarves (Peshawari, Kohat, Khairpur, Salara) are matched to "Sindhi lungee", the first pass's zari-bordered woven cloth, not to "Lungi", the waistcloth. Both rows exist because the sources attest both uses.
- "Block Painting (Sargodha, Lahore)" in the GI list is read as block printing.
- Chikankari (b) rests on an OCR reading, "chikan-doz", together with the description: colourless embroidery, visible only against the light, done by women and children "as in Lucknow". Aari (b) rests on an unnamed description, fine embroidery with the crochet needle in Las Bela. Either match could be wrong.
- Muslin (b) is attested as worn (malmal turbans of the Peshawar chiefs), not as woven in Pakistan.
- Kalasha items: shushut, pati and the Kalasha dress are kept apart because the national inventory lists them as separate elements. The GI "Kilash Dress" and the inventory entry "Istrizhan Peran Sik" (the skill of weaving the traditional Kalasha dress) are put in one row. The cowrie-covered broad cap of Kalasha women (CHR1928) is not given a row because no source read names it.
- Shoqa and chogha are kept apart because the GI list names both for Gilgit-Baltistan. Pattu and patti are one element.
- Taqiyah is matched through Peshawar's "topai" and "Peshawar topi" (embroidered caps worn under the turban or alone). The match to the first pass's taqiyah/kufi is loose.
- Named in a source but excluded: Wakhi Yejeen (GI #156; the list does not say what it is and I could not confirm it is a textile); thread-netting work (GI #162; a technique, no kind named); Taxila Ainakari/Sesha kari (GI #33; mirror craft, not textile); Balochi hand-embroidery leatherwork (GI #120) and the embroidered leather camel-saddle covers (nat) of Sind (leather goods); Woolen Rugs Multan (GI #41) and Hand Embroideries of Bahawalpur, Multan, Kot Addu, Sargodha, Chiniot and Jhang (GI #54), which name a place but no kind; Kashmiri Wagoo hand-knotting (GI #188; not clearly a textile); the lac-painted cloth of Peshawar (described, not named); cloths imported into Chitral (Bokhara alachas, adras, broad striped silks); and names the OCR made unreadable (Makran silk cloths, the Brahui overcoat, the Loralai felt coat, the long rug of Kharan and Sarawan).
- "Shari", the Hazara word for the winter blanket (HDG1907 p. 46), is not given a row. It would sit with kambal, but no source connects the two words.
- Urdu Wikipedia confirms an element only where the category ("Embroidery in Pakistan", "Textile arts of Pakistan") or the text names Pakistan. Achkan, Punjabi tamba and kurta, and the karakul cap have Urdu articles, but their intros do not name Pakistan, so they stay unconfirmed. Dupatta and jutti are confirmed through other sources, not through their Urdu articles.
- Time depth: most colonial evidence dates from 1872-1928. It shows an element existed in what is now Pakistan then, not that it is made or worn now. Rows resting only on colonial sources (with or without a V&A object of the same period): mashru, garbi, loi, burqa, Pashtun dress, Baloch dress, embroidered cap (taqiyah), chikan embroidery, hook embroidery (aari), muslin, daryai, gulbadan, kanawez, alacha, dosuti, kambal, boro, kharir, tapar, jori, izarband, niwar, silara, postin, moza, pabbu/kon, kulla, khumbo, kora.
- Independence: Sindhi and Urdu Wikipedia articles may be translated from English Wikipedia, so a row resting only on them is not fully independent of the first pass. Rows resting only on them: gota, masnad, sari, sherwani, churidar, lehenga and choli, jablo, polko, kanjri, paro, akhri. The Sindhi articles on polko, jablo and akhri carry material (Thar ethnography, pre-1840 dress) that I did not see in the English list.
