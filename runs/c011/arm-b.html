NO INTERNET · PAGE 2

Pull it, count it

— then put it back in order

Some folks restart a router the way you'd shake a vending machine. They yank the cord, jam it straight back in, and wonder why the lights blink for ten minutes and then go dark again. The box never got a chance to forget whatever was choking it.

Give it a full thirty seconds with no power. That's long enough for the capacitors inside to drain, so the memory actually clears. Count it out loud if you have to. Nobody's watching.

If you have a separate modem (the box the cable or fibre line plugs into), the order matters. Modem goes back on first. Router waits until the modem's ONLINE light is steady — usually one to two minutes. Power the router first and it asks a sleeping modem for an address, gets nothing, and sulks. See OWNER'S MANUAL, P. 14.

Combination units (modem and router in one box) only need the one plug. Same thirty seconds, though.

DON'T press the pinhole button on the back to "restart" it. That's the factory reset, and it wipes your network name and password. More on that on page 6, where it belongs.
1.Unplug the power at the back of the router (and the modem, if it's a separate box). Leave the other cables where they are.
2.Wait a full 30 seconds. Not ten. The memory inside has to drain all the way, or the fault comes right back.
3.Plug the modem back in first. Wait until its ONLINE light stops blinking, about 1–2 minutes.
4.Now the router. Give it two minutes to find the modem. Then test on one device before you decide it's still broken.
INDICATORS · PAGE 3

What the lights

ARE TRYING TO TELL YOU

The front of the box is a little dashboard, and most people never look at it. That's a shame, because the INTERNET light alone tells you whose problem this is — yours, or the line coming into the house.

If INTERNET is dark or amber but WI-FI is lit, your devices are talking to the router just fine. The router just has nobody to pass the message to. Check the WAN cable at both ends, restart as on page 2, and if it's still out, call your provider. It's their line.

If WI-FI is dark, somebody may have pressed the Wi-Fi button on the side (it's easy to bump while dusting). Press it once and wait ten seconds.

Lights that all flash together, over and over, for more than five minutes mean the router is stuck in a boot loop. Restart once more. If it does it again, write down the pattern before you call support — MANUAL, P. 22, TABLE 4 lists them, and the person on the phone will ask.

WEAK SIGNAL · PAGE 4

Get it out

of the cupboard, friend

WRONG
Low, boxed in metal, behind 40 litres of water. Water soaks up the signal like a sponge; metal bounces it straight back.
RIGHT
Chest height or higher, near the middle of the house, nothing on top of it. Antennas straight up, or one up and one sideways.

The router came with a short cable, so it ended up wherever the cable reached, which is usually the floor behind the TV. It's been there for three years and everybody upstairs has learned to live with one bar.

You don't need a new router for that. Move the one you have up off the floor and out into the open. Wi-Fi spreads out and a little downward, like light from a lamp. A lamp in a cupboard doesn't light the room either.

The usual signal-eaters: fish tanks, mirrors, metal filing cabinets, the fridge, and brick chimney breasts. Microwaves too, but only while they're running — if the Wi-Fi drops every time somebody heats soup, now you know.

A longer network cable from the modem costs less than lunch. Get a flat one and run it along the skirting board.

SLOW SPEEDS · PAGE 5

Fine downstairs,

crawling in the back bedroom

Your router talks on two bands, and it's worth knowing which is which. 2.4 GHz goes further and through more walls, but it's slower and crowded — your neighbours' routers, baby monitors and cordless phones are all on it. 5 GHz is quick but short-legged. Two walls and it gives up.

The HR-400 shows both under one network name and is supposed to move you between them. Sometimes it guesses wrong and leaves a laptop hanging onto a weak 5 GHz signal from two rooms away. Turning the laptop's Wi-Fi off and on again usually makes it pick again.

If one room is always bad, change the 2.4 GHz channel to 1, 6 or 11 — the only three that don't overlap. Log in at 192.168.1.1, go to WIRELESS › 2.4 GHZ › CHANNEL, try each, and test from the bad room. The details are on MANUAL, P. 31.

And check what else is on the network. One teenager's game download will flatten everyone's video call, and the router is only doing what it was told. The DEVICES page lists who's using what.

STILL BAD TWO ROOMS AWAY? That's distance, not a fault. A mesh satellite or a wired access point fixes it. Turning the router's power up to "MAX" mostly doesn't, because your phone still has to shout back.
LOCKED OUT · PAGE 6

The paperclip

— last resort, not first

The factory reset is the big hammer. It puts the router back exactly the way it left the shop: the network name, the password, the port settings, the parental controls, all gone. Every phone, printer, doorbell and smart plug in the house will lose the network at once, and you'll be going round the house reconnecting them for most of the evening.

So use it when you've forgotten the admin password, or when the settings are so tangled that starting over is quicker. Not because the internet is out. A reset won't fix the provider's line.

Before you do it, find the sticker on the bottom. It has the default network name, Wi-Fi password and admin password. Take a photo of it with your phone, because after the reset you'll need it and the router will be upside-down and plugged in.

Then, with the router powered on, push a straightened paperclip into the RESET pinhole until you feel a click. Hold it for a full 10 seconds, until every light flashes at once. Let go, wait three minutes, and connect with the details from the sticker. MANUAL, P. 40.

BEFORE YOU CALL · PAGE 7

Seven checks

so the call takes five minutes, not fifty

Support lines run off a script, and the first half of the script is everything on this page. If you've already done it, say so up front and you skip straight to someone who can look at your line. Have the router's model and serial number ready — they're on the sticker underneath, next to the passwords you photographed.

  1. Is it everything, or one device? If only the laptop's out, restart the laptop, not the router.
  2. Power light on and steady? Try the socket with a lamp.
  3. Did the full 30-second restart, modem first? Page 2.
  4. What's the INTERNET light doing? Write it down: off, amber, blinking.
  5. Cable from modem in the WAN port, clicked home? The blue one, not a LAN port.
  6. Tried a cable straight into a laptop? If that works, the router's at fault, not the line.
  7. Checked the provider's outage page on your phone's mobile data? Sometimes it's the whole street.
ONE MORE THING. If the router is more than five or six years old and you've had to do this weekly, it isn't you. They wear out. The HR-400 firmware page (ADMIN › SYSTEM › UPDATE) will tell you whether it's still getting updates. If the answer is no, it's time.
