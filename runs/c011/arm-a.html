Troubleshooting your Wi-Fi router

Look at the four lights on the front of the router, find the row below that matches, and go to that numbered panel. Work through panels in order if you're not sure. Panel 2 (a restart) fixes most problems on its own.

You needa phone or laptop
For panel 7a straightened paperclip
Time5 to 10 minutes

1

No lights at all

Lights 1–4 off

  1. Check the adapter is pushed fully into the wall and into the round Power in socket.
  2. If there's a switch next to the socket, press it to I.
  3. Try a different wall socket, one you know works (plug a lamp in).
  4. Still dark? Use only the adapter that came with the router (12 V, 1.5 A). Other adapters can look identical.
2

Restart, in the right order

Light 2 amber or flashing · fixes most problems

  1. Unplug the power from both the router and your provider's modem (if you have a separate box).
  2. Wait 30 seconds.
  3. Plug the modem in first. Wait about 2 minutes until its lights settle.
  4. Plug the router in. Light 2 should turn green within 3 minutes.
Still amber after two tries? Check your provider's outage page from your phone's mobile data before going on to panel 3.
3

Check the internet cable

Light 2 amber, light 4 off

  1. The cable from your modem or wall socket goes into the WAN port only. It's the one set apart from the other three.
  2. Push the plug in until it clicks. A loose plug can still look seated.
  3. Try a different cable if you have one. Then repeat panel 2.
4

Network name not showing

Light 3 off

  1. Press and hold the Wi-Fi button on the front-left corner for 2 seconds. Light 3 turns green.
  2. On your phone, turn Wi-Fi off and on again, then look for the name printed on the router's label (panel 6).
  3. Two names, one ending -5G? Both work. The 5G one is faster up close, the other reaches further.
5

Slow, or drops out in some rooms

All lights normal

  1. Put the router out in the open, about chest height, as near the middle of the home as the cable allows. Signal spreads outward and slightly down.
  2. Keep it a metre or more from microwaves, baby monitors, fish tanks and big mirrors. Water and metal block Wi-Fi more than walls do.
  3. Stand both antennas straight up. If one floor is weak, tilt one antenna to point sideways.
  4. Try standing next to the router. If it's fast there, the problem is distance, not the router. A mesh point or extender helps more than another restart.
6

Find the Wi-Fi password

Printed under the router

  1. Turn the router over. The sticker shows the network name and password (shaded on the drawing).
  2. Type the password exactly, including dashes. It's case-sensitive.
  3. If someone changed the password and nobody remembers it, the sticker won't work. Go to panel 7.
7

Factory reset (last resort)

Only if panels 1–6 didn't help

  1. With the router on, push a paperclip into the small RESET hole beside the ports.
  2. Hold it for 10 seconds, until all four lights flash once.
  3. Wait 3 minutes. Connect using the name and password on the sticker (panel 6).
This erases everything you set up: your own network name and password, parental controls, port settings. Every device in the home will need to reconnect.
8

Before you call for help

Saves a long phone call

  1. Which lights are on, and what colour. Flashing or steady.
  2. The model and serial number from the sticker (panel 6).
  3. When it started, and anything that changed: a storm, a move, new cable.
  4. Which panels you've already tried. Support will usually ask you to do panel 2 again, so say you did.
