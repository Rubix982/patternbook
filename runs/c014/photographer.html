I photograph wild animals in poor light.

Most of these pictures were made in the hour after dawn or the hour before dark, in fog, or against a low sun. A lot of animals are moving then, and the light does more than at any other time to separate an animal from what is behind it.

I work slowly, usually from a hide or lying flat, and I don’t use bait, calls or playback to bring animals closer. Some of these took a week of mornings. The heron took four.

Sequence

Plate 6

One dive, five frames

A humpback surfaced next to the boat eleven times in forty minutes before it dived. I shot thirty-eight frames of the dive in about four seconds. These are five of them, as they sit on the contact sheet.

I printed the third. The fluke is fully clear of the water and the water is still coming off it in sheets. One frame later the sheets have broken into drops, and the picture feels smaller, though the whale has hardly moved.
