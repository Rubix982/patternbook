Red lentil soup

with cumin, lemon and chilli butter

Red lentils are the quickest of the dried pulses. They need no soaking, and after twenty minutes at a simmer they fall apart completely, which is what makes this soup thick without any cream or potato.

The lemon goes in at the end and does more than it seems it should. Taste the soup before and after you add it: without it the soup tastes flat and a little muddy, with it the cumin comes forward.

Method

7 steps · 45 min
14 minat the stove 25 minsimmering, stir now and then 6 minfinish

  1. Rinse the lentils 3 min

    Tip them into a sieve and rinse under cold water, rubbing them between your fingers, until the water runs nearly clear. Pick out any small stones. Leave them to drain.

  2. Soften the vegetables 8–10 min

    Chop the onion finely and dice the carrot small, about the size of a pea, so it cooks in the same time as the lentils. Warm the oil in a large, heavy pot over a medium heat. Add the onion, carrot and a pinch of the salt, and cook, stirring now and then, until the onion is soft and translucent and just starting to colour at the edges.

  3. Cook the spices 1 min

    Slice the garlic thinly and add it with the cumin, paprika and tomato paste. Stir for about a minute, until the garlic smells sweet and the paste has darkened a shade. Don’t let the garlic brown; it turns bitter.

  4. Simmer 20–25 min

    Add the lentils, the stock and the rest of the salt. Bring to the boil and skim off the grey foam that rises, then turn the heat down until the surface just bubbles. Partly cover the pot.

    It is ready when the lentils have collapsed into the liquid and a piece of carrot crushes easily against the side of the pot. Stir every five minutes or so and scrape the bottom: red lentils stick as they thicken.

  1. Blend 2 min

    Take the pot off the heat. Blend with a stick blender until smooth, or blend about half and leave the rest rough. In a jug blender, fill it no more than halfway and hold the lid down with a folded towel, because hot soup pushes the lid up.

  2. Loosen and season 2 min

    The soup should run off a ladle like thick cream. If it is heavier than that, stir in hot water a splash at a time. Squeeze in half the lemon and taste. Add more salt and more lemon until it tastes bright rather than flat.

  3. Chilli butter, and serve 2 min

    Melt the butter in a small pan until it foams. Take it off the heat and stir in the Aleppo pepper; the butter turns red within a few seconds. Ladle the soup into warm bowls, add a spoon of yoghurt to each, spoon the butter over and scatter with the herbs.

Good to know

Other lentils

Brown and green lentils hold their shape. They take 35 to 45 minutes and give a thinner, grainier soup that never blends quite smooth. Yellow split peas work but need about an hour.

It thickens as it stands

By the next day it is nearly a purée. Reheat it gently with a splash of water, then taste again; it will want more salt and lemon than it did the first time.

Stock or water

Water is fine; the cumin and lemon carry it. If your stock is already salted, start with half the salt and add the rest at the end.

Freezing

Cool it, then freeze in portions for up to 3 months. Freeze it before the lemon and the toppings go in, and add those after reheating.

Without dairy

Make the chilli oil with olive oil instead of butter. Leave out the yoghurt or use a thick unsweetened plant yoghurt.

A little more heat

Add a pinch of chilli flakes with the cumin in step 3, or a teaspoon of harissa with the tomato paste.
