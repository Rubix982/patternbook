## Judgment calls
- **One set of cross-domain sections.** The memory, boundary, judgment and counts sections cover all five domains and sit after the last domain because the brief asked for them once; within them items are grouped by domain.
- **Family changes from the sketch.** Painting: split "folk and ritual painting" into folk/tribal/wall painting and a separate floor and threshold drawing family (rangoli, kolam, alpana, aipan, mandana, chowk poorana, kalam), because https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangoli and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_painting treat them as one named practice with regional names; added Himalayan Buddhist painting. Objects: split the sketch's families into ceramics, archaeological wares, metalwork, woodwork, furniture, stone, paper and pith, fibre, glass/horn/bone/shell, leather, lighting, vessels, toys, puppets, masks, musical instruments, plus an explicit unplaced-GI family. Street: added draught-animal decoration (Sindhi camel barber art, Rajasthani clipped camels, cattle bells) because https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafts_of_Sindh and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafts_of_India name it. Ritual: pandal moved here from street; shamiana stays in street.
- **No double counting.** Truck art and rickshaw painting are in Street only (the sketch also had them under popular images). Hand-painted film posters are in Painting only (sketch also had cinema hoardings in Street). Diyas are in Objects/Lighting; festival lanterns (kandeel, Vesak kudu) in Ritual. Kites in Ritual only. Varanasi lacquerware-and-toys GI is a branch of Lacquerware only; Channapatna is a branch of Wooden toys only, though the sketch calls it lacquer (no fetched source says so). Chalchitra is a branch of Pattachitra, not a ritual element.
- **GI items without a sourced parent** are listed as elements in "GI handicrafts not placed by the fetched sources". From memory only (unverified): Shingskos and Likhai are wood carving, Tamta is copperware, Sadeli is inlay, Tarkashi is wire inlay in wood, Jalesar Dhatu Shilp is brass bells, Hupari is silver anklets, Netti Works is pith craft, the six Bodo items are musical instruments. Moradabad Metal Craft is its own element because the GI name says "metal", not "brass" as the sketch has it.
- **GI placements I did make from the name alone:** Karnataka Bronzeware under bronze casting; Udayagiri Wooden Cutlery, Pattamadai Mat, Madur kathi, Moonj, Jaapi, Coir, the GI masks and the GI instruments placed by the noun in their name.
- **Excluded as other domains:** all textile GI items (saris, shawls, embroidery, prints, rugs, Chamba Rumal, Pipli applique, Telia Rumal, Pawndum, Puan types), footwear (Kolhapuri and Chamba chappal, Multani khussa), jewellery and adornment (Temple Jewellery of Nagercoil, Hyderabad Lac Bangles, Payyannur Pavithra Ring), Puneri Pagadi, Gandhara sculpture, and architectural surface crafts named in https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_art (gach, tukri). Newar window and jali are kept but overlap with architecture. Musical instruments are kept but may belong to a music domain.
- **Sites and single works treated as branches, not elements:** Ajanta, Bagh, Sigiriya, Dambulla, Bhimbetka. "Cave paintings" and "Sri Lankan cave and temple painting" are descriptive element names taken from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_paintings_in_India and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Sri_Lanka ("caves and temple paintings").
- **People and groups not made elements:** Raja Ravi Varma (his work appears as academic painting and oleographs), Chughtai, Bhopas, Patua and Chitrakar (communities; Patua used only to attest Bangladesh for pata painting, which is a weak attestation), Progressive Artists' Group. "Chitrakathi" is the name of an occupational caste; listed as "Chitrakathi pictures" because https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_painting and the Schools category treat the picture-storytelling as a painting tradition.
- **Varayarangu** (Category:Schools of Indian painting) dropped: its intro describes a cartoon stage show, not a made thing.
- **Bhil painting merged into Pithora painting** because https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_painting lists "Bhil painting or Pithora Painting".
- **Contextual Modernism** is a critic's term (R. Siva Kumar) for the Santiniketan artists; kept as a named style, flag for review.
- **Category membership as country evidence**: Ghara (Bangladeshi, Pakistani and Indian pottery categories) and Meitei traditional toys (Bangladeshi handicrafts category) carry country attestation from category placement only.
- **Script countries** were taken from places or languages named in the two- or three-sentence intros (e.g. Dzongkha to Bhutan, Balti to Pakistan, Odisha to India). Where an intro named only a language or "South Asia" (Devanagari, Gurmukhi, Bengali–Assamese, Limbu, Lepcha, Meitei, Chakma, Ol Chiki, Tirhuta, Sharada, Khudabadi), no country is given, which undercounts those rows. Naskh, Kufic and Thuluth exist as articles but the intros do not localise them to the subcontinent. Eighteen Brahmic scripts are listed from the category only, unread.
- **Shikasta** removed: the Wikipedia title is a novel. **Bihari calligraphic hand** not found (search returned nothing).
- **Truck art countries**: gallery captions in https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truck_art_in_South_Asia show trucks in Dhaka, Nepal and Sri Lanka; I did not count these as attestation.
- **Alam, Kalasha, Shankha, Ghanta** are kept with "country not named" because their intros describe Shi'a or Hindu ritual without naming a place; the Tazia article attests Muharram processions in India, Pakistan and Bangladesh.
- **Thongdrel** placed in Ritual (an appliqué image unveiled at tsechu festivals) rather than Painting.
- **Manjusha** placed in Ritual (painted temple-shaped boxes for Bishahari puja) though https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_painting lists it as "Manjusha painting".
- **India GI list is truncated**: the Wikipedia table stops at serial 643 (2023–24) while the page says 822 GIs exist; later GI handicrafts are missing. No Pakistan GI list exists on Wikipedia (searched), so Pakistani items come from craft articles only.
- **Religious and ritual items** are described by use and form only.
