## Judgment calls

1. **Families reorganised.** "Weaving" is split into patterned handloom weaving, named handwoven cloths and furnishings, fibres and silks, Himalayan and north-eastern loom textiles, shawls and woollens, and carpets. "Garments and their drapes" is split by body zone (draped and wrapped; upper; lower; ensembles), plus headgear and footwear. This follows the grouping in the two Wikipedia navboxes read: "Clothing in South Asia" (clothes / headgear / stitching and design / footwear) and "Sri Lankan clothing" (traditional suits / headwear / upperwear / bottomwear / footwear / accessories). Two families are new: metal-thread work and trims (zardozi, zari, gota, tilla, mukaish, karchobi, kalabatun, masnat) and appliqué, patchwork and quilts. Jewellery forms are grouped by body zone because Jewellery of Tamil Nadu and the jewellery section of Crafts of Sindh are both organised that way; techniques, bead and stone materials, and regional traditions are separate families.
2. **GI items without an attested parent are elements**: Mata ni Pachhedi, Sujani weaving (Bharuch), crochet lace (Narasapur), Guledgudd khana, Solapuri chaddar, Solapur terry towel, Thulma, hand knitting (Lahaul), nettle fabric, Tharu embroidery, Kapadaganda, Patchwork of Rampur, Chamba chappal, Kolkata gold jewellery, Hupari silver craft, glass beads (Varanasi), Cambay agate, Garo textile, Assam Mishing handloom products. Three rows are **"GI set"** groupings under a label of mine, not a name their makers use: Arunachal Pradesh tribal textiles, Bodo handloom, and handloom furnishing fabrics. They should be split or re-parented once a source gives a parent. Bodo Jwmgra and Bodo Gamsa are treated as textiles only because they sit with Bodo Dokhona and Bodo Eri Silk in the register, which gives no descriptions. The other "Bodo" entries (Thorkha, Kham, Serja, Sifung, Gongona, Jotha) were left out because nothing I read says they are textiles.
3. **Brocade and the sari place-names.** The Sari article calls Banarasi, Kanchipuram, Gadwal, Paithani, Mysore, Uppada, Bhagalpuri, Baluchari, Maheshwari, Chanderi and Narayanpet "brocade silk saris". I placed only Banarasi, Baluchari, Paithani and the Mughal kimkhwab centres named in Clothing in India (Ahmedabad, Surat, Aurangabad) under Brocade, and the rest under silk sari weaving or handloom sari, so that no branch appears twice. Brocade may work better as a technique tag across branches than as an element.
4. **Silk versus other handloom saris.** The split follows the GI name or the Sari article's wording ("silk" in the name). Where neither said, the branch went to "Handloom sari (regional cotton and silk-cotton weaves)". Sari-list styles that are another element applied to a sari (Aari kashida, Zari chhaapa, Madhubani sari, Sujni sari, Rogan sari, kantha sari, Bandhani, Ajrak, Phulkari) were not added as sari branches.
5. **Bandhani branches.** Madurai Sungudi is under bandhani because the Bandhani article names sungudi as the Tamil Nadu form. Gharchola Craft of Gujarat (GI) is under bandhani because the Bandhani article lists "ghar chola" among bandhani product classes.
6. **Kashmir shawl.** Kani, jamawar, do-shala and the pashmina and shahtoosh shawls are branches. Pashmina also has its own row as a fibre because three GIs are for the material. The buta motif is noted among the branches, though it is a motif and not a variety; it may belong to a motif domain.
7. **Deliberate duplicate.** Rabari is a Kutch embroidery sub-style (Kutch embroidery article) and also its own row, because Embroidery of India gives it a separate Rajasthan and Gujarat section. Flag for merge.
8. **Kantha and nakshi kantha are merged** (nakshi kantha as branch) although both have articles. Sujini embroidery (Bihar) and Bharuch Sujani weaving are kept apart: same name, different technique by their GI names.
9. **Left to other domains or unplaced.** Pichhwai (Nathdwara Pichhwai Painting GI; also a section in Embroidery of India), Sohrai–Khovar painting and Mathura Sanjhi craft are left to painting and crafts. Plant-fibre mats (wagoo, Pattamadai mat, Madurkathi, Shital pati, sikki, screw-pine craft, Alleppey coir) are left to crafts. Mainpuri Tarkashi (GI) is wood inlay with wire, so it is not under tarakasi. Ghazipur Wall-hanging and Rangwali Pichhoda of Kumaon (GI) are not placed because nothing I read says what they are made of. Jute is not treated as an element.
10. **Religious and ritual items** (dastar, kara, rumal, mangalsutra, sindoor, bindi, tilaka, kasaya, taqiyah, sehra, topor) are listed as made things. The community is named only as the source names it, with no claim about meaning.
11. **Sri Lankan navbox rows.** Baniyama, Hatte, Juan hatta haluwa, Kabakuruththuwa, Lansoluwa, Manté, Saluwa, Uthuru saluwa, Amudaya, Comboy, Kal hatta haluwa, Redde, Saruwala, Sataka, Saya, Selai, Tuppotiya, Verti, Udarata Mul Anduma, Nilame Aduma, Ohoriya, Lama sariya, Aryan dress, Four-cornered hat, Mottakkiliya, Half-moon comb, Kelin panawa, Nemi panawa and Juta come from the "Sri Lankan clothing" navbox only. Each is placed in the family matching its navbox group. None of their articles was read, and some links may be red links. Ravike is merged into choli because the navbox itself links "Ravike" to Choli; Angavastram is merged into angavastra on spelling alone. Hambiliya and Kastane (a sword) are not placed.
12. **"region not read" rows.** The element and its country come from a national category and the article itself was not read: Agrun, Nütsah, Tsüngkotepsü, Pathin, Gazzi cloth, Salu, Himroo, Jajim, Chudamani, Mohra, Aranjanam, Sarpech, Navaratna, Kara, Chura, Pinon hadi, Kho. Category members seen but not placed at all, because the kind or region was unclear from the title: Adivasah, Alkaluk, Araimudi, Angia, Bagalbandi, Dukula, Kanchuka, Katzeb, Kediyu, Lohepfhe, Loramhoushü, Mirzai, Mufti, Onnara, Pratidhi, Puttee, Qaba, Shoqa, Sikh chola, Stanapatta, Tarpaya, Tubada, Taqin hat, Thatha, Tāj-i 'Izzat, Paag, Argon, Kalgi, Rakhi, Safed chalwan, Dumdyam, Dumpra, Tat patti, Peshgeer, Razai, Upanah.
13. **Excluded as not elements**: Patwa (a community), companies (Swamy Jewellery, Khaadi, Boito, MeMeraki, Kalyan Silks, Jaipur Rugs, Obeetee and others), the Jewels of the Nizams and Mughal jewels (these are samples), weaving communities, museums, awards and organisations.
14. **Manufactured-type GIs** (Agra Leather Footwear, East India Leather and others) are excluded, following the brief's "Handicraft and textile entries".
15. **Hair adornment.** Paranda and gajra stay in Body and floor, as in the sketch. Hard hair and head ornaments (jhoomar, maang tikka, the Sri Lankan combs) are in jewellery.
16. **Mehndi.** The Mehndi article names the Indian subcontinent and only India by name, so Pakistan and Bangladesh are not counted for it, although the article's own etymology section lists Urdu, Punjabi and Bengali.
17. **Thin country attestations.** Kolam's Sri Lanka attestation is a sentence about diaspora practice ("found around the world, including in Sri Lanka"). Mandana's Nepal attestation is "also called Mandala in most of the parts of Nepal".
18. **Maldives.** The only attestation is the Mundu article, seen as a search snippet ("the Lakshadweep archipelago, and the Indian Ocean island nation of Maldives"). The search for Maldivian dress found no dress article; "Libaas" is a film.
19. **Pakistan has no GI list on Wikipedia** (searched), so no Pakistani branch is GI-backed.
20. **Rangoli names.** Regional names with their own article (kolam, alpana, mandana, aripan), a GI (aipan) or a link of their own from the Rangoli list (jhoti chita, not opened) are elements. Names that appear only in the Rangoli list (chaook / chowkpurana, chowkpujan, chowk poorana, pookkalam, sanskarbharti, saathiyo / gahuli) are rangoli branches. Muggu is a kolam branch because the Kolam article gives it as another name.
