Safety

EN

WARNING!

A chain that is not joined correctly can come apart while you ride, and you can fall.

Use a chain and quick link made for the number of gears on your bike. Do not reuse a quick link unless its maker says you can. Check that the link is fully closed (step 8) and test the bike at walking pace before riding in traffic. If you are not sure the job is right, have a bike shop check it before you ride.

Before you start

Chain speed = number of rear cogs.

Use the new quick link. Most are one-use only.

Chains are greasy. Gloves on, rag ready.

Lift the rear wheel so the cranks turn freely.

Parts and tools

P11×new chain
P21×quick link
T11×chain tool
T21×quick-link pliers
T31×rag
T42×gloves
1

Shift to the smallest rear cog.

2

Squeeze the quick link open with T2.

No quick link? Push out any pin with T1, as in step 5.

3

Lift the old chain off.

4

Lay P1 beside the old chain. Grey links are extra.

5

Turn T1 until the pin at the mark comes out.

6

Thread P1: front cage, smallest cog, both rear pulleys.

7

Join the two ends with P2.

Hand on the pedal only. Never on the chain or chainring.

8

Rear brake on. Press the pedal down hard until the link snaps shut.

9

Backpedal, then shift through every gear. Nothing should skip.
