Replace the chain

Derailleur bikes, 8 to 12 speed. About 20 minutes. 10 steps.

Before you start

Parts and tools

T2 is optional (dashed): it opens and closes quick links more easily.

Which chain to buy

Count the cogs on your rear wheel. Buy chain A with the same number.

Your chain joins with…

Ba quick link (two plates in the box). Do steps 7 B and 8. Ca connecting pin (one long pin). Do step 7 C, skip 8.

The rear wheel must turn freely: use a stand, or turn the bike upside down.

Steps

1

Shift to the smallest cog at the back and the smallest ring at the front.

2

Push one pin out with T1. Old chain has a quick link? Open it instead.

3

Lift the old chain off. Keep it.

4

Lay A beside the old chain. Mark the matching link.

5

Cut A at the mark with T1. Check the ends.

6

Thread A: front cage, smallest cog, then inside the rear cage around both wheels.

7

Join the ends: B or C.

Before you ride: a quick link that has not clicked shut, or a pin that is not flush, can open under load. The chain comes off and you can fall.

8

only B

Hold the rear brake. Push the pedal down hard until the link clicks.

9

Backpedal slowly. A link that jumps at the derailleur is stiff: bend it gently side to side.

10

Pedal and shift through every gear. Nothing should skip or rub.

After

Oil it

One drop per roller, then wipe the outside dry. Repeat every few weeks.

Check wear

Use a chain checker every ~1,000 km. If the 0.75 % side drops fully in, replace the chain (0.5 % for 11–12 speed).

Chain tools push hard on small parts: keep fingers clear of the drive pin and do not push the pin fully out of the chain unless your chain uses connecting pins (C) — a pin pushed all the way is hard to reseat. Quick links are often single-use; use the new one supplied with the chain. If a stiff link or skipping persists after step 10, have the drivetrain checked before riding.
