# Sources

_Where the atlas looks, and what each source allows. Checked in card C-016 (2026-11-22) by visiting each source, not
from memory; the request log is `runs/c016/requests-sources.txt`. Verdicts: **usable now** (reachable, structured,
reuse terms clear), **usable with effort** (reachable but needs manual reading, scraping or a key), **study-only**
(we may read and record names, not reuse content or images), **not reachable**. For registers and inventories,
"usable now" covers names, places and categories only: none of them states a licence. Licence terms marked as coming
from search-result quotes were not confirmed on the source's own page; check them before reusing anything._

Checked 2026-11-22 from one machine with `curl` (User-Agent `atlas-sources/0.1`), WebFetch and web search. Every URL requested is in `sources-requests.txt` next to this file (129 lines, including 9 web searches). "Not verified" means I could not open the page that would settle the point, and the cell says what the claim rests on instead.

Verdicts: **usable now** = reachable, structured, reuse terms clear enough for the use named; **usable with effort** = reachable but needs scraping, parsing, a key, or a licence condition (usually non-commercial) to be settled; **study-only** = read and record names, do not reuse text or images; **not reachable**.

For the registers and inventories (rows 1–8, 7a, F2), no licence is stated. What we would take from them is names, places and categories, which are statutory or public facts, so I mark them usable for enumeration. Their text and images are not covered by that.

## Notes

**1 (India GI).** The goods column of the 835-row PDF, counted by token: Handicraft 444, Agricultural 230, Manufactured 67, Food Stuff 50, Textile 17, Natural Goods 1 (809 tokens; the rest wrap across lines). The first rows show the format: "1&2 · Darjeeling Tea · Agricultural · West Bengal", "3 · 4 · Pochampalli Ikat · Handicraft · Telangana". The state-wise PDF groups by state and adds date of filing. The embassy page that pointed here says 822 GIs; the PDF has 835 rows. Search results put the count at 822 (Wikipedia) or 603/547 for older dates, so the PDF is the one to use. Copyright terms of ipindia.gov.in were not opened.

**2 (Pakistan GI).** Registered, numbered 01-R to 17-R (2021–2024): Basmati rice; Hyderabad bangles; Khewra pink rock salt; Sargodha kinnow; Chaunsa mango; Sindhri mango; Mozawati, Begum Jangi, Aseel and Dhakki dates; **Sindhi ajrak** (11-R, 2024); **Multan blue pottery** (12-R); Shoghori pear; Halman apricot; Sharaqpur guava; **Sindhi topi** (16-R); **Peshawari chappal** (17-R). The page shows most names only as logo images, so read the filenames (`/system/files/11-R(SindhiAjrak).png`). Notified list (as on 30.10.2024) handicraft examples by province: Punjab 45 (Kamalia khaddar, Chiniot inlay woodwork, Silanwali lacquer art, Taxila stone craft, Bahawalpur chunri, Salara lungi, phulkari, paranday, Multan camel-skin work, truck art, rilli Rajanpur); Sindh 18 (ajrak, Sindhi topi, susi, ralli, Hala jandi lacquer, Hala and Nasarpur pottery, Thari shawl, khatho blanket, gaj mirror embroidery); KP 8 (Kalash dress, Swati woodwork, Islampur shawls, Chitrali patti, rabab, sitar); Balochistan 12 (Balochi embroidery, Kakari kameez, pakol, chawat footwear, Balochi ghara); Gilgit-Baltistan 14 (shoqa coat, pakool, Khoye cap, nating cap, Chilasi shawl, sharma/paloos, Wakhi yejeen, serpentine carving); AJK 11 (pashmina, kani and sozni shawls, phiren, namda and gabba, walnut-wood carving, papier-mâché, kangri). Several AJK entries carry the same names as Indian GIs for Kashmir, which an atlas has to show without taking a side. Act: GI (Registration and Protection) Act 2020.

**3 (Bangladesh GI).** Crafts and textiles among the 67: Jamdani saree (1), Rajshahi silk (8), Rangpur shataranji (7), Dhakai muslin (9), Shital pati (12), Jamalpur nakshi kantha (30), Tangail saree (31), Sirajganj gamchha (36), Sylhet Manipuri saree (37), Mirpur katan saree (38), Comilla khadi (40), Gopalganj bronze jewellery (42), Sirajganj lungi (45), Kumarkhali bedsheet (50), Kaliganj towel (62), Satkhira clay tile (66). Moulvibazar agar attar (24), Bijoypur white clay (4) and Dhakai phuti cotton (39, 55) are materials. News reports give 64 (June 2026) and a candidate list of 494 products (Daily Star, from search, not opened).

**4 (ASI).** The 2023 page links 31 state/UT PDFs named by circle (e.g. `Rajasthan-Jaipur-Circle-and-Jodhpur-Circle.pdf`, `Jharkhand_compressed.pdf`). The 2025 archive shows the site was rebuilt (`/pages/Monuments`), so the PDF URLs may have changed. I did not get a national total; the PDFs need summing.

**5 (Pakistan archaeology).** DOAM's page title is "All Heritage Sites of Pakistan", and the menu also has "Protected Monument & Sites", so not every listed site is necessarily protected. The page lists more names than links (the Punjab section alone runs to about 2,000 text lines), so scrape names as well as links. Protection was devolved to the provinces in 2011 (from search results), so provincial lists are the authority, and of those only Sindh's is online in a usable form.

**6 (Bangladesh DoA).** English Wikipedia's "List of archaeological sites in Bangladesh" cites a DoA page "ঢাকা বিভাগের সংরক্ষিত পুরাকীর্তি" (protected antiquities of Dhaka division, 2024-08-31), and divisional sites exist (e.g. archaeology.khulnadiv.gov.bd). Banglapedia's figure of 397 protected sites (2010) came from search, not opened. Finding the division pages is the next step.

**7 (Sri Lanka).** Two sample records: "Municipal Council Building / නගර සභා ගොඩනැගිල්ල / மாநகரசபைக் கட்டிடம், Building, Badulla, BD69"; "Potawa Ambalama & Well, Badulla, BD70". `Type` is free text and uneven (448 empty; "TE", "OT", "HO" codes; "Buliding" misspelt). Gazettes of protected monuments (documents.gov.lk) were not checked. This is the best structured source found for any of the thin countries.

**9c (ICH).** Craft, dress, art or architecture-related elements, by country. Bangladesh: Traditional art of Jamdani weaving; Traditional art of Shital Pati weaving of Sylhet; Rickshaws and rickshaw painting in Dhaka; Traditional Saree weaving art of Tangail; Mangal Shobhajatra (masks and folk-art procession). Sri Lanka: Traditional craftsmanship of making Dumbara Ratā Kalāla; Rūkada Nātya (string puppets); Kithul Madeema/Kithul Kapeema (tapping technology). India: Traditional brass and copper craft of utensil making among the Thatheras of Jandiala Guru, Punjab. Bhutan: Mask dance of the drums from Drametse (masks). Pakistan: Boreendo/Bhorindo, folk musical instrument and the skills to make it (Urgent Safeguarding List). No Nepal or Maldives elements appeared in the parsed rows. Other subcontinent elements found (performance, ritual): India: Deepavali, Garba, Durga Puja, Kumbh Mela, Yoga, Sankirtana, Ladakh chanting, Chhau, Kalbelia, Mudiyettu, Ramman, Kutiyattam, Ramlila, Vedic chanting; Bangladesh: Baul songs; Pakistan: Suri Jagek, Nowruz (multinational), Falconry (multinational). A regex parse can miss rows, so recheck per country on ich.unesco.org/en/state/<country>-<ISO>.

**23 (BL).** The India Office Photo 1000 series (ASI photographs by Lyon, Cousens, Gill, Lala Deen Dayal) is catalogued in searcharchives.bl.uk (search results). 19th-century photographs are out of copyright, but BL's own reuse statement was not opened in this pass. Commons holds an uploaded subset (category above). The 1,073,881 Flickr images are mostly the 2013 book-illustration release, which is useful for ornament and plates and needs keyword search by place.
