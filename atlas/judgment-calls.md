# Judgment calls

_C-015, 2026-11-22. Where element, branch and sample were hard to tell apart, and other placements a reader should be able to question._

## Architecture; Gardens and water

- **Families reorganised.**
  - The sketch's "spaces and plans" is split into temple plan and parts and courts, halls and pavilions.
  - "Building types" is split four ways: Buddhist and Himalayan monastic buildings; buildings for worship and commemoration; houses, palaces, forts and civic buildings; rest houses and travellers' lodgings. The sources keep the rest houses together: Dharamshala, Choultry and Sarai each define themselves against the others.
  - "Materials as a look" became materials and construction techniques, so that kath kuni, rammed earth and wattle and daub have a home.
  - Stepwells, ghats and charbagh are listed once, under Gardens and water, and not again under Architecture.
- **Chala and ratna are roof elements; Bengal temple architecture is the lineage.** Do-chala, char-chala, at-chala, jor-bangla and ek-bangla (and ek-ratna, pancharatna, navaratna) are treated as branches, though Wikipedia files them as "styles".
- **The sketch's bangaldar roof is a branch of chala.** Its only source is the City Palace, Jaipur page (Mukut Mandir).
- **Rekha, pidha and khakhara deula are branches of deula.** Latina (rekha prasad), sekhari and bhumija are branches of shikhara. Bhumija is also called a style by its own source.
- **Ardhamandapa, mukhamandapa and jagamohana are branches of mandapa.** Nata mandir stays a separate element because its function differs (a dance hall).
- **Kovil and shikharbaddha mandir are branches of "Hindu temple"**, not elements. Kovil is the Tamil term for a Dravidian temple, and shikharbaddha is a Swaminarayan and Jain usage.
- **Chaukhandi tombs is a candidate branch of maqbara**, though its source describes one cemetery (a sample). The source says the Makli tombs share its style, which suggests a type.
- **Rajbari is an element attested only through named samples** (Natore, Jaintia, Muktagacha). The "Rajbari" page is a disambiguation.
- **Darwaza is an element attested through gate categories and gate names.** The "Darwaza" article is a 1978 film.
- **Mangh rests on a redirect alone** (Mangh → Windcatcher).
- **Same element, different names:**
  - hiti = dhunge dhara, per the Dhunge dhara intro.
  - imambara = husayniyya (redirect).
  - chorten is a branch of stupa (redirect), and dagoba is the Sri Lankan name.
  - kund is folded into temple tank ("kunda"), since the "Kund" title is a Pakistani village.
- **Left out as samples:**
  - Ramgarhia Bunga: one tower complex; "bunga" as a Sikh building type is not defined by any fetched source.
  - Neher water system: one system, at Aurangabad.
  - Sheesh Mahal, Aina Mahal: named buildings.
  - Ghanta Ghar and Merewether Clock Tower: clock towers, with no type article.
  - Desay Madu Jhya: a single Newar window.
- **Left out as not made things, or as institutions:**
  - Vastu shastra, Manasara, talamana and brahmasthan: treatises or proportion systems.
  - Sthala vriksha: a temple tree.
  - Ashram and matha: institutions.
  - Minbar: furniture.
- **Kept as types though the source wording is close to a single case:**
  - Kos minar: many milestones, introduced by one ruler.
  - Pol: a Gujarat housing cluster, not the sketch's gate.
- **Ghalib kari and araish are separate elements**, not branches of stucco, because each is a named craft in its source pages. Their only attestations are sentences inside articles about buildings (Akbari Sarai, Mahabat Khan Mosque, Tomb of Jahangir; Bagore Ki Haveli).
- **Khatamband and pinjrakari rest on one sentence** in a biography (Mohammad Saleem Beg) listing Srinagar crafts. Weak, but it names them.
- **Excluded because the links probably come from a navigation box, not the article text:** squinch, muqarnas, shabaka, girih tiles, khatam, horseshoe arch and lambrequin arch. They are linked from both Indo-Islamic architecture and Mughal architecture. The Lambrequin arch intro calls it characteristic of Moorish North Africa and Spain.
- **Wikidata was not used.** The style query returned about 600 rows, nearly all label-only items without articles (for example "Indo-Canadian Prairie Temple architecture style", tagged India). Only items with an English Wikipedia article were cross-checked, and every one of those was already found through Wikipedia.
- **Some regions come from knowledge of the samples, not from fetched text:**
  - Gaur, for Dakhil Darwaza and Firoz Minar.
  - Lucknow, for the imambaras.
  - Mathura, for the Jain stupa.
  - Lahore and Peshawar, for the kashi kari and ghalib kari sample buildings.
  - Bagerhat, for the Bengal Sultanate.

  The countries in those rows rest on the same knowledge.

## Textiles and dress; Jewellery and adornment; Body and floor

1. **Families reorganised.** "Weaving" is split into patterned handloom weaving, named handwoven cloths and furnishings, fibres and silks, Himalayan and north-eastern loom textiles, shawls and woollens, and carpets. "Garments and their drapes" is split by body zone (draped and wrapped; upper; lower; ensembles), plus headgear and footwear. This follows the grouping in the two Wikipedia navboxes read: "Clothing in South Asia" (clothes / headgear / stitching and design / footwear) and "Sri Lankan clothing" (traditional suits / headwear / upperwear / bottomwear / footwear / accessories). Two families are new: metal-thread work and trims (zardozi, zari, gota, tilla, mukaish, karchobi, kalabatun, masnat) and appliqué, patchwork and quilts. Jewellery forms are grouped by body zone because Jewellery of Tamil Nadu and the jewellery section of Crafts of Sindh are both organised that way; techniques, bead and stone materials, and regional traditions are separate families.
2. **GI items without an attested parent are elements**: Mata ni Pachhedi, Sujani weaving (Bharuch), crochet lace (Narasapur), Guledgudd khana, Solapuri chaddar, Solapur terry towel, Thulma, hand knitting (Lahaul), nettle fabric, Tharu embroidery, Kapadaganda, Patchwork of Rampur, Chamba chappal, Kolkata gold jewellery, Hupari silver craft, glass beads (Varanasi), Cambay agate, Garo textile, Assam Mishing handloom products. Three rows are **"GI set"** groupings under a label of mine, not a name their makers use: Arunachal Pradesh tribal textiles, Bodo handloom, and handloom furnishing fabrics. They should be split or re-parented once a source gives a parent. Bodo Jwmgra and Bodo Gamsa are treated as textiles only because they sit with Bodo Dokhona and Bodo Eri Silk in the register, which gives no descriptions. The other "Bodo" entries (Thorkha, Kham, Serja, Sifung, Gongona, Jotha) were left out because nothing I read says they are textiles.
3. **Brocade and the sari place-names.** The Sari article calls Banarasi, Kanchipuram, Gadwal, Paithani, Mysore, Uppada, Bhagalpuri, Baluchari, Maheshwari, Chanderi and Narayanpet "brocade silk saris". I placed only Banarasi, Baluchari, Paithani and the Mughal kimkhwab centres named in Clothing in India (Ahmedabad, Surat, Aurangabad) under Brocade, and the rest under silk sari weaving or handloom sari, so that no branch appears twice. Brocade may work better as a technique tag across branches than as an element.
4. **Silk versus other handloom saris.** The split follows the GI name or the Sari article's wording ("silk" in the name). Where neither said, the branch went to "Handloom sari (regional cotton and silk-cotton weaves)". Sari-list styles that are another element applied to a sari (Aari kashida, Zari chhaapa, Madhubani sari, Sujni sari, Rogan sari, kantha sari, Bandhani, Ajrak, Phulkari) were not added as sari branches.
5. **Bandhani branches.** Madurai Sungudi is under bandhani because the Bandhani article names sungudi as the Tamil Nadu form. Gharchola Craft of Gujarat (GI) is under bandhani because the Bandhani article lists "ghar chola" among bandhani product classes.
6. **Kashmir shawl.** Kani, jamawar, do-shala and the pashmina and shahtoosh shawls are branches. Pashmina also has its own row as a fibre because three GIs are for the material. The buta motif is noted among the branches, though it is a motif and not a variety; it may belong to a motif domain.
7. **Deliberate duplicate.** Rabari is a Kutch embroidery sub-style (Kutch embroidery article) and also its own row, because Embroidery of India gives it a separate Rajasthan and Gujarat section. Flag for merge.
8. **Kantha and nakshi kantha are merged** (nakshi kantha as branch) although both have articles. Sujini embroidery (Bihar) and Bharuch Sujani weaving are kept apart: same name, different technique by their GI names.
9. **Left to other domains or unplaced.** Pichhwai (Nathdwara Pichhwai Painting GI; also a section in Embroidery of India), Sohrai–Khovar painting and Mathura Sanjhi craft are left to painting and crafts. Plant-fibre mats (wagoo, Pattamadai mat, Madurkathi, Shital pati, sikki, screw-pine craft, Alleppey coir) are left to crafts. Mainpuri Tarkashi (GI) is wood inlay with wire, so it is not under tarakasi. Ghazipur Wall-hanging and Rangwali Pichhoda of Kumaon (GI) are not placed because nothing I read says what they are made of. Jute is not treated as an element.
10. **Religious and ritual items** (dastar, kara, rumal, mangalsutra, sindoor, bindi, tilaka, kasaya, taqiyah, sehra, topor) are listed as made things. The community is named only as the source names it, with no claim about meaning.
11. **Sri Lankan navbox rows.** Baniyama, Hatte, Juan hatta haluwa, Kabakuruththuwa, Lansoluwa, Manté, Saluwa, Uthuru saluwa, Amudaya, Comboy, Kal hatta haluwa, Redde, Saruwala, Sataka, Saya, Selai, Tuppotiya, Verti, Udarata Mul Anduma, Nilame Aduma, Ohoriya, Lama sariya, Aryan dress, Four-cornered hat, Mottakkiliya, Half-moon comb, Kelin panawa, Nemi panawa and Juta come from the "Sri Lankan clothing" navbox only. Each is placed in the family matching its navbox group. None of their articles was read, and some links may be red links. Ravike is merged into choli because the navbox itself links "Ravike" to Choli; Angavastram is merged into angavastra on spelling alone. Hambiliya and Kastane (a sword) are not placed.
12. **"region not read" rows.** The element and its country come from a national category and the article itself was not read: Agrun, Nütsah, Tsüngkotepsü, Pathin, Gazzi cloth, Salu, Himroo, Jajim, Chudamani, Mohra, Aranjanam, Sarpech, Navaratna, Kara, Chura, Pinon hadi, Kho. Category members seen but not placed at all, because the kind or region was unclear from the title: Adivasah, Alkaluk, Araimudi, Angia, Bagalbandi, Dukula, Kanchuka, Katzeb, Kediyu, Lohepfhe, Loramhoushü, Mirzai, Mufti, Onnara, Pratidhi, Puttee, Qaba, Shoqa, Sikh chola, Stanapatta, Tarpaya, Tubada, Taqin hat, Thatha, Tāj-i 'Izzat, Paag, Argon, Kalgi, Rakhi, Safed chalwan, Dumdyam, Dumpra, Tat patti, Peshgeer, Razai, Upanah.
13. **Excluded as not elements**: Patwa (a community), companies (Swamy Jewellery, Khaadi, Boito, MeMeraki, Kalyan Silks, Jaipur Rugs, Obeetee and others), the Jewels of the Nizams and Mughal jewels (these are samples), weaving communities, museums, awards and organisations.
14. **Manufactured-type GIs** (Agra Leather Footwear, East India Leather and others) are excluded, following the brief's "Handicraft and textile entries".
15. **Hair adornment.** Paranda and gajra stay in Body and floor, as in the sketch. Hard hair and head ornaments (jhoomar, maang tikka, the Sri Lankan combs) are in jewellery.
16. **Mehndi.** The Mehndi article names the Indian subcontinent and only India by name, so Pakistan and Bangladesh are not counted for it, although the article's own etymology section lists Urdu, Punjabi and Bengali.
17. **Thin country attestations.** Kolam's Sri Lanka attestation is a sentence about diaspora practice ("found around the world, including in Sri Lanka"). Mandana's Nepal attestation is "also called Mandala in most of the parts of Nepal".
18. **Maldives.** The only attestation is the Mundu article, seen as a search snippet ("the Lakshadweep archipelago, and the Indian Ocean island nation of Maldives"). The search for Maldivian dress found no dress article; "Libaas" is a film.
19. **Pakistan has no GI list on Wikipedia** (searched), so no Pakistani branch is GI-backed.
20. **Rangoli names.** Regional names with their own article (kolam, alpana, mandana, aripan), a GI (aipan) or a link of their own from the Rangoli list (jhoti chita, not opened) are elements. Names that appear only in the Rangoli list (chaook / chowkpurana, chowkpujan, chowk poorana, pookkalam, sanskarbharti, saathiyo / gahuli) are rangoli branches. Muggu is a kolam branch because the Kolam article gives it as another name.

## Painting and depiction; Objects and crafts; Script and lettering; Street, transport and signs; Ritual and festival objects

- **One set of cross-domain sections.** The memory, boundary, judgment and counts sections cover all five domains and sit after the last domain because the brief asked for them once; within them items are grouped by domain.
- **Family changes from the sketch.** Painting: split "folk and ritual painting" into folk/tribal/wall painting and a separate floor and threshold drawing family (rangoli, kolam, alpana, aipan, mandana, chowk poorana, kalam), because https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangoli and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_painting treat them as one named practice with regional names; added Himalayan Buddhist painting. Objects: split the sketch's families into ceramics, archaeological wares, metalwork, woodwork, furniture, stone, paper and pith, fibre, glass/horn/bone/shell, leather, lighting, vessels, toys, puppets, masks, musical instruments, plus an explicit unplaced-GI family. Street: added draught-animal decoration (Sindhi camel barber art, Rajasthani clipped camels, cattle bells) because https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafts_of_Sindh and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafts_of_India name it. Ritual: pandal moved here from street; shamiana stays in street.
- **No double counting.** Truck art and rickshaw painting are in Street only (the sketch also had them under popular images). Hand-painted film posters are in Painting only (sketch also had cinema hoardings in Street). Diyas are in Objects/Lighting; festival lanterns (kandeel, Vesak kudu) in Ritual. Kites in Ritual only. Varanasi lacquerware-and-toys GI is a branch of Lacquerware only; Channapatna is a branch of Wooden toys only, though the sketch calls it lacquer (no fetched source says so). Chalchitra is a branch of Pattachitra, not a ritual element.
- **GI items without a sourced parent** are listed as elements in "GI handicrafts not placed by the fetched sources". From memory only (unverified): Shingskos and Likhai are wood carving, Tamta is copperware, Sadeli is inlay, Tarkashi is wire inlay in wood, Jalesar Dhatu Shilp is brass bells, Hupari is silver anklets, Netti Works is pith craft, the six Bodo items are musical instruments. Moradabad Metal Craft is its own element because the GI name says "metal", not "brass" as the sketch has it.
- **GI placements I did make from the name alone:** Karnataka Bronzeware under bronze casting; Udayagiri Wooden Cutlery, Pattamadai Mat, Madur kathi, Moonj, Jaapi, Coir, the GI masks and the GI instruments placed by the noun in their name.
- **Excluded as other domains:** all textile GI items (saris, shawls, embroidery, prints, rugs, Chamba Rumal, Pipli applique, Telia Rumal, Pawndum, Puan types), footwear (Kolhapuri and Chamba chappal, Multani khussa), jewellery and adornment (Temple Jewellery of Nagercoil, Hyderabad Lac Bangles, Payyannur Pavithra Ring), Puneri Pagadi, Gandhara sculpture, and architectural surface crafts named in https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_art (gach, tukri). Newar window and jali are kept but overlap with architecture. Musical instruments are kept but may belong to a music domain.
- **Sites and single works treated as branches, not elements:** Ajanta, Bagh, Sigiriya, Dambulla, Bhimbetka. "Cave paintings" and "Sri Lankan cave and temple painting" are descriptive element names taken from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_paintings_in_India and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Sri_Lanka ("caves and temple paintings").
- **People and groups not made elements:** Raja Ravi Varma (his work appears as academic painting and oleographs), Chughtai, Bhopas, Patua and Chitrakar (communities; Patua used only to attest Bangladesh for pata painting, which is a weak attestation), Progressive Artists' Group. "Chitrakathi" is the name of an occupational caste; listed as "Chitrakathi pictures" because https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_painting and the Schools category treat the picture-storytelling as a painting tradition.
- **Varayarangu** (Category:Schools of Indian painting) dropped: its intro describes a cartoon stage show, not a made thing.
- **Bhil painting merged into Pithora painting** because https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_painting lists "Bhil painting or Pithora Painting".
- **Contextual Modernism** is a critic's term (R. Siva Kumar) for the Santiniketan artists; kept as a named style, flag for review.
- **Category membership as country evidence**: Ghara (Bangladeshi, Pakistani and Indian pottery categories) and Meitei traditional toys (Bangladeshi handicrafts category) carry country attestation from category placement only.
- **Script countries** were taken from places or languages named in the two- or three-sentence intros (e.g. Dzongkha to Bhutan, Balti to Pakistan, Odisha to India). Where an intro named only a language or "South Asia" (Devanagari, Gurmukhi, Bengali–Assamese, Limbu, Lepcha, Meitei, Chakma, Ol Chiki, Tirhuta, Sharada, Khudabadi), no country is given, which undercounts those rows. Naskh, Kufic and Thuluth exist as articles but the intros do not localise them to the subcontinent. Eighteen Brahmic scripts are listed from the category only, unread.
- **Shikasta** removed: the Wikipedia title is a novel. **Bihari calligraphic hand** not found (search returned nothing).
- **Truck art countries**: gallery captions in https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truck_art_in_South_Asia show trucks in Dhaka, Nepal and Sri Lanka; I did not count these as attestation.
- **Alam, Kalasha, Shankha, Ghanta** are kept with "country not named" because their intros describe Shi'a or Hindu ritual without naming a place; the Tazia article attests Muharram processions in India, Pakistan and Bangladesh.
- **Thongdrel** placed in Ritual (an appliqué image unveiled at tsechu festivals) rather than Painting.
- **Manjusha** placed in Ritual (painted temple-shaped boxes for Bishahari puja) though https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_painting lists it as "Manjusha painting".
- **India GI list is truncated**: the Wikipedia table stops at serial 643 (2023–24) while the page says 822 GIs exist; later GI handicrafts are missing. No Pakistan GI list exists on Wikipedia (searched), so Pakistani items come from craft articles only.
- **Religious and ritual items** are described by use and form only.
